# Decision-Maker Access Playbook
## How Brian Gets In Front of Workforce Decision Makers Without Cold Email

**Last updated:** 2026-04-07
**Author:** LMT marketing system

---

## The Principle

Cold email and cold calls have a 1-2% response rate. Everything in this playbook is built around a different rule:

> **Show up where they already are. Be visible before you ask.**

By the time you ask for the meeting, the decision maker should already recognize your name from 3+ touchpoints. That's the difference between a 2% reply rate and a 30% reply rate.

---

## The 16 Channels (in order of effort vs payoff)

### Tier A — Highest leverage, lowest cost

#### 1. Local government public meetings (your "city hall" instinct is right — formalize it)
- **Austin City Council** — meets Thursdays. Public comment is open. Register 24 hrs in advance. You get 2-3 minutes at the podium. Workforce items are usually on the agenda monthly.
- **Travis County Commissioners** — Tuesdays. Same drill.
- **Workforce Solutions Capital Area Board** — meets monthly. ALL meetings are open to the public per Texas Open Meetings Act. Show up. Take notes. Introduce yourself to board members during break.
- **Texas Workforce Commission** quarterly meetings — Austin HQ. Public.
- **Austin Independent School District board** — when CTE / workforce items come up.

**Action:** Put all 5 on your calendar as standing recurring events. Attend ONE per week minimum. Bring printed one-pagers about LMT (no pitch — just "we exist, here's the data"). Hand them to specific board members when you introduce yourself after the meeting.

#### 2. Workforce board service
- **Apply to serve** on a workforce board, advisory committee, or city commission related to aging, workforce, or digital equity.
- **Targets:** Workforce Solutions Capital Area Youth Council, Mayor's Committee on People with Disabilities (workforce angle), Austin Commission on Aging, Greater Austin Hispanic Chamber Workforce Committee.
- **Why it works:** Instant credibility, instant network, you're "in the room" by definition.

**Action:** Pick one. Apply this month. Your MBE certification + Pioneer data + book make you a strong candidate.

#### 3. Strategic LinkedIn comment hijacking
- Find the 5-10 most influential workforce voices in Texas (Workforce Solutions ED, TWC commissioners, big-employer CHROs, AARP Texas director).
- Comment thoughtfully on EVERY post they make for 30 days.
- Use the 5 reply patterns from the morning routine.
- They will recognize your name. Their followers will recognize your name. By day 30, you can DM them and they know who you are.

**Action:** Build the list this week. Add to morning routine.

#### 4. Reciprocity-first social plays
- Quote their work in your LinkedIn post (with permission or attribution).
- Tag them when you reference their org's data.
- Amplify their wins publicly (congratulate them on their LinkedIn).
- Send them a "I shared your work today" DM with the link.

**Action:** Pick one Tier 1 person every Friday and do this. Make a list, work the list.

#### 5. Op-eds in local publications
- **Austin American-Statesman** — Opinion section. They publish workforce/business op-eds. 600-800 words.
- **Austin Business Journal** — Workforce/HR coverage. Pitch them story ideas.
- **Texas Tribune** — Workforce policy angle. They love data-driven contrarian takes.
- **Dallas Morning News, Houston Chronicle, San Antonio Express** — same model in other markets.
- **Local TV news** — KXAN, KEYE, KVUE, FOX 7. They need workforce experts. Pitch yourself.

**Action:** Write ONE op-ed this month. The $850B series gives you 4 different angles. "Workers 55-64 stay 10.1 years — here's what we're losing" is a publishable headline.

### Tier B — Medium leverage, medium cost

#### 6. Speaking circuit (the long game)
- **Apply to speak at:** Austin Tech Council, AustinCC events, Texas SHRM, ATD Austin, Texas Workforce Symposium, Texas Conference for Women, AARP events, MBE Roundtable, Greater Austin Black Chamber, Austin Asian American Chamber.
- **Strategy:** Speak free at small events (Rotary, Lions, Kiwanis, Chamber breakfasts) to build the reel. Each free talk = 2-3 high-value contacts in the room. Within 6 months, charge $1-2K. Within 12, $5K+.

**Action:** Apply to 3 speaking opportunities this week. Even small ones. Use the morning routine's research section to find them.

#### 7. Podcast guest appearances
- **Targets:** HR podcasts, workforce podcasts, AI training podcasts, MBE business podcasts, age-inclusion podcasts, Texas business podcasts.
- **Pitch:** "I have data nobody else has on adults 50+ in tech training (3X completion, 74% confidence). Would your audience benefit from a contrarian take on workforce age bias?"
- **Goal:** 2 podcast appearances per month. Each one = ~500-2000 new ears + a piece of evergreen content + a backlink to your site.

**Action:** Build a list of 20 target podcasts. Pitch 5 this week.

#### 8. Strategic partnerships with adjacent orgs
- **Co-host events with:** Austin Community College Continuing Education, AARP Texas, the Austin Public Library system (digital literacy programs), Capital IDEA (workforce training nonprofit), Goodwill Central Texas.
- **Why:** Their email list becomes accessible to you. Their members trust them. You inherit that trust.

**Action:** Pick ONE partner. Pitch a co-hosted free workshop this month.

#### 9. Awards and nominations
- **Apply for / nominate yourself for:** Austin Under 40, MBE of the Year (regional), Greater Austin Hispanic Chamber Awards, Tech Diversity Pledge recognition, AARP Purpose Prize, Heinz Awards, James Beard-style awards in workforce education.
- **Why:** Award winners get press. Press gets leads.

**Action:** Find 3 awards this month. Apply to one.

#### 10. Workshops at libraries, senior centers, churches
- **Targets:** Austin Public Library locations (free workshop programs already exist), Senior Services of Austin, AGE of Central Texas, faith communities (Black church networks, Hispanic Catholic networks).
- **Why:** Direct Pioneer recruitment + word of mouth + visible community impact = ammunition for grant applications and decision-maker pitches.

**Action:** Pitch one location per week for a free 30-min workshop. Use your existing curriculum.

### Tier C — High leverage, higher effort

#### 11. Host your own event: "50+TechBridge Demo Day"
- Half-day event at a community space (library or community college conference room — usually free or cheap).
- Invite all Tier 1 prospects + media.
- Format: 3 Pioneers tell their story (10 min each), Brian gives a 20-min keynote with data, Q&A, hors d'oeuvres, networking.
- Media plays: invite local TV, business journal reporter, op-ed columnists.
- Cost: ~$500-1000 (food + space + printed materials).
- Output: professional video footage, photos for social, press coverage, 50-100 new connections, 10+ direct conversations with decision makers.

**Action:** Plan one for July or August. Start the invite list this week.

#### 12. The "Custom Data Brief" approach (the ultimate cold-email kill)
- Pick a Tier 1 organization (e.g., a specific Texas Workforce Commission program).
- Spend 4-6 hours researching their public data: their annual report, their RFPs, their staff list, their stated workforce goals, any audits or evaluations.
- Build a 3-page custom brief: "Here's what your workforce demographics look like, here's where you have a 50+ gap, here's the ROI model if you trained 200 of them."
- Send it to the decision maker by mail (physical, with a handwritten note) or hand-deliver.
- Response rate: ~40-60% (vs 2% cold email).

**Action:** Pick one org per quarter. Build the brief. Hand-deliver it.

#### 13. Letters of support from current Pioneers
- Ask 5 of your strongest Pioneers to write a 1-page letter to a specific decision maker (you provide the recipient + a template).
- These get read. Personal stories from constituents are worth 100x cold emails.
- Use for: workforce board members, city council, state reps, federal grant officers.

**Action:** Set up a Pioneer Advocate Program. Ask the next time you're with a class.

#### 14. LinkedIn Live + Newsletter (the authority compound interest play)
- **LinkedIn Live monthly:** 30-min interview with a workforce expert (one of your Tier 1 contacts — they get exposure, you get credibility, the recording becomes content).
- **LinkedIn Newsletter:** Weekly, 800-1200 words, data-driven workforce insights. Subscribers get notified directly — bypasses the algorithm. Higher engagement than regular posts.

**Action:** Launch the newsletter this month. Title: "The Workforce Math" or "The 850 Billion Newsletter".

### Tier D — Lower priority, but worth knowing

#### 15. Twitter/X workforce policy crowd
- Workforce policy people live on Twitter/X more than LinkedIn.
- @ColumbusWorkforce, @USDOL, @EconomicPolicy, @CenterAmericanProgress, regional workforce reporters.
- Lower priority because it's a smaller audience but the audience is highly concentrated decision makers.

#### 16. Federal grant officer relationships
- WIOA money flows from federal → state → local. The federal grant officers and program managers at U.S. DOL Employment & Training Administration are real people who fund things they understand and trust.
- Build relationships at federal workforce conferences (NAWB, USCM, Conference of Mayors).

If you only have 30 days to test all of this, here's the order:

**Week 1**

**Week 2**
- [ ] Attend ONE local government meeting in person
- [ ] Submit op-ed to 2 publications
- [ ] Apply to one workforce board / advisory committee
- [ ] Pitch 5 podcasts

**Week 3**
- [ ] Attend ONE more meeting
- [ ] Pitch 3 speaking opportunities
- [ ] Schedule one library/senior center workshop for May
- [ ] Identify partner for co-hosted event

**Week 4**
- [ ] Build the Tier 1 prospect list (50 names) using the prospect-research skill
- [ ] Send 5 personalized comment-first warm-ups
- [ ] Launch LinkedIn Newsletter (post 1)
- [ ] Plan Demo Day (date, venue, invite list)

**End of 30 days, you should have:**
- 4-5 in-person government meetings attended
- 1 op-ed submitted (maybe published)
- 1 workforce board application in
- 5 podcast pitches sent (1-2 will land)
- 3 speaking pitches sent
- 1 workshop scheduled
- 50 prospects fully researched + warmed
- 5 LinkedIn DMs to people who already recognize you
- 1 newsletter live
- 1 demo day on the calendar

That's how you go from "I'm going to start emailing people" to "I'm in the room every week."

## What to NEVER do

- ❌ Buy a list of HR emails and blast a sequence
- ❌ Use a sales automation tool that sends 50 LinkedIn requests/day (LinkedIn jail)
- ❌ Pitch in the first connection request
- ❌ Lead with the ask. Always lead with value or a question.
- ❌ Email senior executives without 3+ prior touches
- ❌ Send the same generic message to 20 people
- ❌ Forget to follow up. Most deals close on touch 5-7, not touch 1.

## How to use this playbook with the morning routine

The Marketing Morning Routine trigger handles the *daily* execution of channels 3, 4, and parts of 5 (LinkedIn engagement + prospecting + research). This playbook covers the *weekly and monthly* moves the morning routine can't automate.

**Pair them:**
- Daily: morning routine (LinkedIn + prospect adds + research)
- Weekly: pick 1-2 Tier B/C plays from this doc
- Monthly: 1 Tier C play (Demo Day, custom brief, op-ed)
- Quarterly: review what worked, kill what didn't

Run the prospect-research skill (`/prospect [name]` or `research [name]`) on every new contact. Keep dossiers in `marketing/prospects/`.

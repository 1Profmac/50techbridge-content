## PART 1 — How small companies actually get into the WIOA / 50+ workforce game

### The 4 entry paths (ranked by speed, not by size of prize)

#### Path 1 — Subgrantee under a national funder (FASTEST: 2–4 months)
Big national grantees (AARP Foundation, CWI Works, SER Jobs, NCOA) receive federal money that they're *required* to push out to community partners. They WANT small specialized providers. You don't compete with AARP — you become *part of* what AARP funds.

**How to enter:**
1. Identify the program (Digital Skills Ready@50+, BACK TO WORK 50+, SCSEP)
2. Apply to be a community partner via their partnership form or phone (AARP: 1-855-850-2525)
3. Pass their basic vetting (insurance, references, outcomes data, MBE/501c3 nice-to-have)
4. Sign a subgrant agreement
5. Start serving participants within 30–60 days

**What they require from a small provider:**
- ✅ A defined program with clear curriculum
- ✅ Outcome data (LMT has 347+/3X/74% — already meets bar)
- ✅ Insurance ($1M general liability typical)
- ✅ Financial stability (1+ year operating history, basic financials)
- ✅ References from prior clients (3+ named)
- ✅ Ability to report participant data in their format
- ❌ NOT required: ETPL listing, federal contractor registration, deep WIOA expertise

#### Path 2 — Operations contractor partnership (FAST: 1–3 months)
C2GPS, ResCare, Equus, and Eckerd all run multiple WDBs and are constantly shopping for training providers that can deliver outcomes inside their existing contracts. This is the fastest scaling path because *one* contract can put you in front of *multiple* boards instantly.

**How to enter:**
1. Identify the contractor's Business Services lead (Jason Helmick at C2GPS, etc.)
2. Pitch your program with your outcome numbers
3. Get added to their preferred provider list
4. They route eligible participants to you within their existing service area

**What they require:**
- ✅ Outcome metrics that make THEM look good to the WDB
- ✅ Co-branding flexibility (their name on your materials)
- ✅ Standardized data export (PIRL or their own format)
- ✅ Contractor-friendly cost structure
- ✅ Insurance + W-9
- ❌ NOT required: ETPL (they handle that for you in some cases)

#### Path 3 — Direct ETPL listing (SLOW: 6–12 months)
The "official" path. Each state has its own ETPL application process. In Texas, providers apply through TWC, demonstrate WIOA Title I alignment, prove outcomes, get listed.

**How to enter:**
1. Read the TWC ETPL application requirements
2. Compile your performance data in PIRL format (Participant Individual Record Layout — federal data standard)
3. Submit application + supporting docs
4. Wait for state review (3–6 months typical)
5. Get listed
6. WDBs can then fund individual training accounts (ITAs) through your program

**What they require:**
- ✅ Detailed performance data history
- ✅ Federal data reporting capability (PIRL)
- ✅ State-specific application paperwork
- ✅ Curriculum that maps to WIOA Title I outcome criteria
- ✅ Pricing transparency
- ✅ Organizational stability

**This is the slowest but highest-leverage path long-term.** Once you're on the ETPL, every one of the 28 Texas WDBs can fund your students without further approval.

#### Path 4 — Direct foundation grant (VARIABLE: 3–18 months)
Skip the federal pipeline entirely. Apply to foundations whose mission you fit.

**How to enter:**
1. Identify foundations with explicit 50+ digital skills priority (Google.org, AARP Foundation, JPMorgan Chase, Walmart)
2. Build a one-page concept memo
3. Email program officers directly OR submit through their LOI (letter of inquiry) process
4. If invited, submit full proposal
5. Receive grant or rejection in 3–9 months

**What they require:**
- ✅ Mission alignment story
- ✅ Theory of change
- ✅ Outcome data (LMT has this)
- ✅ Budget transparency
- ✅ 501(c)(3) status OR fiscal sponsor (LMT is for-profit MBE — needs a fiscal sponsor for most foundation grants)
- ⚠️ The 501(c)(3) gap is the biggest barrier for LMT specifically. Solutions: partner with a nonprofit fiscal sponsor (e.g., a community college foundation or AARP Foundation itself).

This is your *value proposition* to a contractor or sub-grantee partner. You're not asking them for help — you're solving their problems.

|---|---|---|
| **Their existing 50+ programs have 30% completion rates** | LMT delivers 90%+ (3X industry rate) | Higher completion = better WIOA outcomes = more federal $$ next year |
| **They have no AI fluency curriculum** — most "digital literacy" stops at email and Word | LMT is built around ChatGPT, Claude, AI tools | AI is the #1 demand from employers right now |
| **Their 50+ learners feel stigmatized in mixed-age classrooms** | LMT is purpose-built for adults 50+, not "adapted from" | Better learner experience = better retention = better outcomes |

| **They struggle to recruit 50+ participants** | LMT generates inbound interest from the 50+ audience directly | You bring the learners; they fund them |
| **They need outcome data they can show their funders** | LMT tracks completion, confidence, employer engagement | Plug-and-play reporting |
| **They're scared of "ageism" controversy** | LMT's "I Fight For You" framing turns ageism into a strength story, not a liability | Risk management |

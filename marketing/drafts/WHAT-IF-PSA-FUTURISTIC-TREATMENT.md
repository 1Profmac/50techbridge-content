## THE REFERENCE: APPLE "1984"

Director Ridley Scott shot this the year after Blade Runner. It is not a commercial.
It is a film about oppression and liberation — told in 60 seconds.

**What makes it work:**

- **Grey conformist masses** marching in lockstep, identical, vacant
- **Cold industrial dystopia** — brutalist tunnels, smoke, backlighting, fog
- **One rebel in color** — woman in red shorts, white top, cuts through grey like a signal
- **Authority as a giant screen** — talking head, totalitarian control, no one questioning
- **The act of defiance** — she doesn't argue. She doesn't petition. She throws the hammer.
- **The screen shatters** — conformity breaks. Everything changes. One person did it.

The text at the end: *"You'll see why 1984 won't be like '1984.'"*

**Our text:** *"You're not done yet."*

Same structure. Same energy. Different enemy.

## VISUAL LANGUAGE — FUTURISTIC TREATMENT

### Color Architecture (Two Worlds)

**WORLD 1 — The Corporate Machine (0:00–2:30)**
- Palette: Cold steel blue, silver-grey, harsh white fluorescent, deep black
- Accent: Data-red for algorithm screens, metrics, "PROCESSING" labels
- Human skin tones: desaturated, slightly drained
- Lighting: Top-down harsh white. No warmth. No shadows that comfort.
- Texture: Glass. Metal. Polished floors. The human form reflected, fragmented.

**THE BREAK — The Turn (2:30)**
- One frame: darkness
- Next frame: a single warm light source — like a candle, like a match
- Not grand. Not explosive. Just: warmth existing where cold was.

**WORLD 2 — The Human Answer (2:33–4:50)**
- Palette: LMT deep navy warming to gold
- Accent: Gold text, warm laptop glow, natural light from windows
- Human skin tones: restored — warm, present, vivid
- Lighting: Side light. Natural. The kind that makes a face look real.
- Texture: Wood tables. Worn hands. Real rooms. Lived-in.

---

### Cinematography Rules

- **Slow motion used as weight** — not beauty. When a face takes in bad news: slow it down. Make the audience sit in it.
- **The march effect** — wide shots of workers moving in sync, shot from a low angle looking up. Like the 1984 tunnel sequences.
- **Haze and backlight** — in corporate/machine scenes, slight atmospheric haze with hard backlight. Workers silhouetted against glowing screens.
- **The single focus pull** — in machine world, background sharp/foreground blurred. In human world, reverse. The machine world cannot see the individual. The human world can only see them.
- **Framing the face** — in the corporate world, faces are never fully in frame. Cut off at the forehead. Cropped at the jaw. Partial. Dehumanized. In the human world: full frame. Centered. Eye level.
- **The Authority Screen** — lit from below, like a news anchor crossed with Big Brother. Sterile. Clinical. Never warm.

---

### Typography — On-Screen Text Treatment

**Machine World text:** Sans-serif. All caps. Data-display font (like Eurostile or OCR-A). White on black or red on black. Appears as if generated, not designed — like a readout.

**Human World text:** Serif warmth. Mixed case. Gold on navy. Appears as if written, not generated.

**The contrast is intentional.** The machine world generates data. The human world writes meaning.

**Opening title cards (before voiceover):**

```
[Black screen]

PROCESSING...

STATUS: WORKFORCE OPTIMIZATION IN PROGRESS

PROJECTED HEADCOUNT REDUCTION: 34%

EFFICIENCY SCORE: BELOW THRESHOLD

RECOMMENDED ACTION: REPLACEMENT

[3-second hold]

[Cut to: City. Cold morning. The march begins.]
```

### SCENE 4 — 1:00 | THE INTERFACE NO ONE BUILT FOR HER
**[INT. PHARMACY — DAY. NEAR-FUTURE.]*

She looks at the screen. The screen does not look back.

She walks away without her medication.

Behind her: the automated system continues processing.

**VOICEOVER:**
*What if the systems built to serve you — were actually built with you in mind.*
*Not as an afterthought. Not buried in accessibility settings.*
*From the start.*

**VOICEOVER:**

*And 75 million people weren't treated as an afterthought —*
*in a world their taxes built, their labor built, their sacrifice built.*

### SCENE 6 — 1:42 | THE MACHINE DECIDES YOUR FUTURE

A manager presents to a room.

The camera pans slowly.

### SCENE 7 — 2:10 | RAPID MONTAGE — THE SYSTEM IN ACTION
**[20 SECONDS. MACHINE-WORLD MONTAGE. FAST CUTS.]*

3. A tech startup lobby. Ping pong table. Median age clearly 27. Holographic product board on the wall. The target market segment box: 18–35.
4. An AI dashboard auto-populating replacement job listings. The listed salary for the replacement role: 40% less than the person being replaced.

**VOICEOVER:**

### SCENE 8 — 2:30 | THE BREAK
**[CUT TO: BLACK. THREE FULL SECONDS. SILENCE.]*

This is the moment.

Like the frame before the woman in 1984 releases the hammer.

The audience doesn't know what's coming. The machine world just... stops.

### SCENE 9 — 2:33 | THE TURN — ONE SMALL ROOM
**[INT. COMMUNITY CENTER — AUSTIN, TEXAS. THE WARMTH RETURNS.]*

Everything changes.

Not with an explosion. With a room.

Folding tables. Laptops that have scratches. Fluorescent light that is somehow warm because of the people under it.

Adults 50, 58, 64, 71 — leaning forward. Typing.

The camera finds a woman's hands. She types something into an AI interface. Waits.

Reads the result.

Her face does something the machine world never showed us.

She smiles. Not for the camera. The kind that comes from understanding something you didn't expect to understand — and suddenly knowing you are more capable than someone else's story about you.

**[MUSIC SHIFTS. PIANO ENTERS. MAJOR KEY. FIRST WARMTH.]*

**VOICEOVER:**
*We asked that question.*
*Every version of it.*
*And then — we stopped waiting for an answer.*

Galaxy Prompt:
```
Community center interior, Austin Texas, daytime. Real working space — not sleek. Folding tables, basic laptops, fluorescent light. Six adults aged 50 to 71, diverse, seated around tables with laptops open. They are engaged — leaning forward, some typing, one asking a neighbor a question. Close-up: a Black woman, 64, types something into an AI chat interface on her laptop. She reads the response. Her face transforms — genuine surprise, then a real smile. Not polite. The kind of smile that comes from realizing something about yourself that someone else had told you wasn't there. Warm lighting from a window to the left. The room feels lived in, used, real. Not a stock photo. Shot from medium distance, intimate.
```

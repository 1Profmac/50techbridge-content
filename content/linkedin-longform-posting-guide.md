# How to Post Long-Form WordPress Articles on LinkedIn

A practical guide for getting your long-form content from WordPress onto LinkedIn — without getting rejected, throttled, or buried by the algorithm.

---

## The Core Problem

LinkedIn has **two completely different systems** for content, and most people don't realize they're separate:

### 1. Regular feed posts
- **Hard cap: 3,000 characters** (roughly 500 words)
- This is what's blocking your long pastes
- "Long-form posts" that experts praise in 2026 are *long within this 3,000-character limit* — dense, emotional, packed writing squeezed into that ceiling

### 2. LinkedIn Articles (a separate feature)
- Up to **110,000 characters** — basically unlimited
- Lives at a different URL, looks like a blog post
- Accessed by clicking **"Write article"** (not the regular post box)
- Different algorithm — gets less feed reach but stays searchable forever

---

## What "Long-Form Post" Actually Means in 2026

When LinkedIn experts say "long-form posts work," they mean **packed, dense posts that fill the 3,000-character window** — not 1,500-word essays.

The format that wins:

```
Hook (1 line — bold claim)

Setup (2-3 short lines)

The story or argument 
(short paragraphs, lots of line breaks)

The turn / insight

The point

Call to action / question
```

**Rules of thumb:**
- Average sentence length: 8–12 words
- Average paragraph: 1–3 lines
- White space everywhere
- Line breaks force the eye to slow down → boosts dwell time

## Why LinkedIn Rejects Your Posts

Common reasons LinkedIn blocks or fails to publish:

1. **Over 3,000 characters** — most likely cause for long pastes
2. **Hidden characters from copy/paste** — pasting from Word, Google Docs, or WordPress adds invisible formatting characters that count against your limit
3. **Link/URL preview issues** — long URLs with tracking parameters can get flagged
4. **Hashtag overload** — more than ~5 hashtags can trigger spam filters

## The Four Ways to Get a WP Article onto LinkedIn

### Option 1: Share the WP Link as a Regular Post
**Easiest, weakest reach**

Just paste the WordPress URL into a LinkedIn post. LinkedIn auto-generates a preview card.

**The catch:** LinkedIn's algorithm penalizes posts with external links because they pull users off the platform. Your post will get 30–50% less reach.

**Workaround:** Post the article *without* the link, then drop the link in the **first comment**. This bypasses most of the penalty.

---

### Option 2: Republish as a LinkedIn Article
**Best for SEO + permanence**

Copy your WordPress article, paste it into LinkedIn's native **Article** editor.

**How to find it:**
- Go to your LinkedIn home feed
- Click "Start a post"
- Look for **"Write article on LinkedIn"** at the top of the post window
- This opens a full blog-style editor — title, hero image, formatted body
- Up to 110,000 characters, full formatting, embedded images

**Why this works:**
- Lives on LinkedIn permanently and shows up on your profile
- Searchable inside LinkedIn and on Google
- Builds your "thought leader" footprint
- No external link penalty

**The catch:** Articles get less feed reach than posts. They're a slow-burn play, not a today-spike.

**SEO consideration:** Google may treat your LinkedIn copy as a duplicate of your WP version. Three ways to handle:
- Rewrite the LinkedIn version (different opening, condensed middle, same core ideas)
- Wait 1–2 weeks after the WP version is indexed before posting on LinkedIn
- Add a line at the top: *"Originally published on learnmoretechnologies.com"* with the link — LinkedIn allows this and preserves SEO authority for your site

---

### Option 3: Native PDF Carousel
**Highest engagement format in 2026**

Take the article, break it into 8–12 slides in Canva (one idea per slide, big text), export as PDF, upload as a "Document" post on LinkedIn.

**Why it works:**
- Native documents (carousels/PDFs) are the #1 LinkedIn format in 2026
- 7% engagement rate, up 14% year-over-year
- People swipe through → dwell time goes up → algorithm boosts it
- End the carousel with "Read the full article →" and put the WP link in the post text or first comment

---

### Option 4: Long Teaser Post + Link in Comments
**Best for driving WP traffic**

Copy the strongest **2,500 characters** from your article, paste as a regular LinkedIn post. End with:

> *"The full piece is in the first comment ↓"*

Drop the WP link in comment #1.

This combines feed reach (the post is link-free) with click-through (engaged readers click for more).

## Recommended Strategy: Use One Article Three Ways

For each WordPress article, run this rotation:

| Day | Action | Goal |
|-----|--------|------|
| **Day 1** | Native PDF carousel (Option 3) | Biggest engagement spike, get eyeballs |
| **Day 3–4** | Long teaser post + link in first comment (Option 4) | Drive WP traffic |
| **Week 2** | Republish as LinkedIn Article (Option 2) | Long-term searchable footprint |

**Result:** One article powers three weeks of LinkedIn presence and reaches different parts of the algorithm.

## The Critical Copy/Paste Fix

When you copy from WordPress, you bring **invisible HTML and formatting** that breaks LinkedIn. Always paste through a plain-text cleaner first:

**On Mac:**
1. Copy from WordPress
2. Paste into TextEdit
3. Format → Make Plain Text
4. Copy from TextEdit
5. Paste into LinkedIn

**On Windows:**
1. Copy from WordPress
2. Paste into Notepad
3. Copy from Notepad
4. Paste into LinkedIn

**Quick trick (any OS):**
- Paste into the URL bar of your browser
- Then copy it back out
- This strips all formatting

LinkedIn's editor will then let you re-add bold, bullets, etc. cleanly.

Key benchmarks to keep in mind:

- **Impressions are down 63–66% since 2023** across the platform — this isn't you, it's the algorithm
- **Engagement per post is up 12–39%** — fewer people see your stuff, but those who do interact more
- **Dwell time matters most:** posts with 61+ seconds dwell time get 15.6% engagement vs 1.2% for posts under 3 seconds
- **Comments carry 15x algorithmic weight** vs likes
- **Shares: 5x weight. Saves: 3x weight**
- **Average engagement rate: 3.85% for personal profiles** — 4–6% is good, 8%+ is excellent

**Format winners in 2026:**
- 🥇 Native PDF documents (7% engagement)
- 🥈 Multi-image posts
- 🥉 Long-form text (within 3,000 chars)
- ❌ Video views down 36% YoY across all page sizes

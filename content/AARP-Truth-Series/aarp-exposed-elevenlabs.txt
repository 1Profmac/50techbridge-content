AARP Exposed the Problem. We Drive the Solution.

By Professor Brian McKinney. Founder, Learn More Technologies.

I worked at AARP. It's where I learned that the data on older adults and technology isn't just alarming. It's actionable.

AARP's research team spent three years documenting exactly how America is failing its most experienced citizens. The numbers are precise. The trends are undeniable. But research doesn't train people. Programs do.

Someone has to take what AARP published and put it to work in senior centers, libraries, and churches where the people actually are. That's what I built 50 Plus Tech Bridge to do.

Here's what the data says.

Forty-seven percent of workers over 50 want AI training. Only ten percent have received it. Employers will spend four thousand dollars to recruit a replacement. They won't spend five hundred to train the person who's been there twenty years.

AI usage among adults fifty and older went from nine percent to thirty percent in two years. They're adopting faster than any other demographic, with zero institutional support.

Adults sixty and older lost seven point seven billion dollars to fraud last year. Up sixty percent in one year. The people most targeted by digital fraud are the ones who received the least digital training. Then the programs designed to protect them were defunded. They cut the shield and blamed the victim.

Sixty percent of adults over fifty say technology is not designed with them in mind. This number hasn't moved in three years. Not one percentage point.

Nineteen million adults sixty-five and older don't have broadband at home. The two federal programs that helped are gone. And the industry that profits from connectivity hasn't said a word.

Forty percent of adults forty-five and older are lonely. Up from thirty-five percent in 2018. Despite nine in ten being on social media. We connected everyone. And everyone feels more alone.

Adults fifty and older spent ninety-one billion dollars on technology last year. They control seventy-six trillion in wealth. The marketing industry targets them with five percent of its budget. The math is not complicated. Somebody just isn't doing it.

Sixty-four percent of workers over fifty have experienced age discrimination. Meanwhile, workers fifty and older listing AI skills on LinkedIn grew twenty-five percent in five years. They're upskilling. The bias isn't moving.

Sixty-three million Americans are caregivers. Fifty-five percent use technology to coordinate care. Nobody trained them. The most tech-dependent unpaid workforce in America has a training budget of zero.

Every one of these problems has something in common. The data exists. AARP did the hard part. They proved the need. But forty-seven percent of workers over fifty are still waiting for someone to show up with a laptop and say, let me teach you.

Research proves the need. Grassroots community development meets it.

I took what I learned at AARP and built the field program the data demanded. The people we serve aren't facing one barrier. They're facing four at once. Age. Race. Sex. And the systematic defunding of the community support resources they've relied on for decades.

This isn't a technology problem. It's an intersection of every structural failure in the system happening at the same time to the same people.

You don't solve that with a report. You solve it by showing up. In person. Where the people are.

Senior centers. Because that's where seventy-year-olds eat lunch on Tuesday and nobody else is offering to teach them anything. Libraries. Because they're free, they're trusted, and they have wifi. Churches. Because faith communities host fellowship lunches on Saturday and Sunday. Kitchen tables. Because sometimes a sixty-five-year-old woman just wants someone to sit across from her and show her how to Face Time her grandkids.

Our completion rate is three times the industry average. Seventy-four percent report higher confidence. Twenty-three organizations invited us in. Three hundred and forty-seven Pioneers trained and counting. The foundational digital skills lessons are free. Always have been. Always will be.

The model is called Each One Teach One. When Shirley learns AI on Tuesday, she teaches Marcus at church on Thursday. By the end of the month, one lesson has reached twelve people. Adults over fifty don't need to be talked down to. They need to be trusted with the information. Then they do what they've always done. They pass it on.

AARP exposed the problem. We drive the solution. A hybrid tech and touch approach to digital inclusion. Hard to duplicate. Easy to support.

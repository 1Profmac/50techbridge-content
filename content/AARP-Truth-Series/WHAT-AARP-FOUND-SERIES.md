And nobody in tech said a word.

Because the tech industry doesn't need 75-year-olds in rural Mississippi to hit their quarterly numbers. They need 25-year-olds in San Francisco.

## POST 8 — The Workplace Bias

64% of workers over 50 have seen or experienced age discrimination.

33% say coworkers assume older employees are less tech-savvy.

These numbers haven't changed in two years.

Here's what changed: workers 50+ listing AI skills on LinkedIn grew 25% in five years. Nearly double the growth rate of younger workers.

They're upskilling faster than any other demographic. And the bias isn't moving.

Because this was never about skills. It was about assumptions.

The same worker who is "too old to learn AI" has 30 years of client relationships, institutional knowledge, and operational judgment that no 25-year-old can replicate.

BCG just published a report saying 43% of companies need generalists who can manage human-agent teams. McKinsey says the new roles are "orchestrators" and "hybrid managers."

That's a job description for experience. Not youth.

Stop hiring for potential. Start valuing what's already proven.

## POST 9 — The Caregiver Burden

63 million Americans are caregivers.

1 in 4 adults. Up nearly 50% since 2015.

55% use technology to coordinate care. Scheduling. Medication tracking. Doctor portals. Insurance forms. Video calls with specialists across state lines.

Nobody trained them for any of it.

They figured it out in crisis mode. At 2 AM. While their parent was in the hospital and the pharmacy portal wouldn't load.

The average caregiver is 49 years old. They're managing their parent's health, their child's schedule, and their own career — all on a phone screen nobody designed for multitasking under stress.

This is the most tech-dependent unpaid workforce in America. And the training budget for them is zero.

At 50+TechBridge, we train the caregiver too. Because when Mom learns to manage her own health portal, the daughter doesn't have to drive 45 minutes to do it for her.

Independence isn't just a value. It's a load off somebody else's back.

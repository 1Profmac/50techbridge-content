[TITLE CARD: Why Your 50+ Workforce Is Your Biggest Untapped Asset]

[SEGMENT 1 — WHAT "UNTAPPED" MEANS | ~60 seconds]
[GRAPHIC: "Untapped = Value Present, Not Extracted"]

"When I say untapped, I am not talking about
corporate social responsibility or inclusion initiatives.

I am talking about return on investment.

Untapped means there is value present
that is not being extracted.

And the value in your experienced workforce is substantial,
specific, and measurable — if you know where to look.

Institutional knowledge that took decades to accumulate.
Professional networks built over 30-year careers.
Judgment — the kind that comes from having seen
a situation go wrong before and course-corrected
before the crisis arrived.

None of that shows up on a skills assessment.
All of it is real. All of it is valuable.
And in most organizations — it is being systematically wasted."

---

[SEGMENT 2 — THE NUMBERS | ~90 seconds]
[GRAPHIC: "What You're Not Tracking"]

"Ask most HR leaders what their most valuable workforce assets are —
they'll point to data.

Ask them what the 50+ segment contributes to those metrics —
most won't have a ready answer.

That is the problem. You cannot optimize for what you're not measuring.

Here's what the research shows when organizations actually look:

Workers over 50 have retention rates three times higher
than workers aged 25 to 34.

If it costs your organization $15,000 to $25,000 to replace
an entry-level employee —
the experienced worker who stays is generating that savings
every single year just by showing up.

Lower absenteeism. Stronger performance consistency.
Greater institutional loyalty.

These are not soft benefits. These are operational advantages —
measurable, trackable, and real."

---

[SEGMENT 3 — THE TECH MYTH | ~90 seconds]
[GRAPHIC: "The Primary Objection"]

"I keep hearing the same objection when I talk to workforce leaders:

'They don't adapt to technology as quickly.'
'Digital transformation is harder with older workers.'
'We need people who are comfortable with AI.'

Here's what I know from actually training 347 adults over 50
in digital and AI skills:

The technology gap is not a capability gap.
It is an investment gap.

My program's completion rate is three times the industry average.
74% of participants feel significantly more confident with technology.
23 organizations have invited us in.

And here's what makes experienced workers different
when they learn a new tool:

They don't use it the way a 28-year-old uses it.

They use it with 25 years of domain expertise
behind every single prompt.

They know which questions to ask
because they've spent three decades learning
what the right questions are.

That combination — deep expertise plus new tool —
is not just comparable to what a younger colleague produces.

In many cases it is better."

---

[SEGMENT 4 — THE CONCRETE PICTURE | ~60 seconds]
[GRAPHIC: "What The Untapped Asset Actually Looks Like"]

"Let me make this concrete.

Think about the employee in your organization
who has been there the longest.

What does she know that is not documented anywhere?

Which vendor relationship was damaged three years ago — and why.
Which regulatory interpretation cost the organization money in 2019.
Which process works in theory and breaks down in practice.

That knowledge is not in any system.
It is not in any handbook.

It is in her.

Now imagine what happens when she retires —
or when the organization's failure to invest in her
pushes her out before she's ready to go —
without any process to transfer what she knows.

Organizations call this a knowledge transfer problem.

It is actually a retention problem.
And it is almost always a leadership problem."

---

[SEGMENT 5 — THE MENTOR MULTIPLIER | ~60 seconds]
[GRAPHIC: "The Multiplier Effect"]

"The experienced worker is not just an asset in herself.
She is a multiplier for everyone around her.

Most organizational learning doesn't happen in formal training programs.

It happens in the informal mentorship relationships
between experienced employees and newer ones.

When those relationships work —
a new hire becomes effective in months instead of years.

When they don't exist — because the experienced workforce
has been neglected or pushed out —
the informal learning infrastructure collapses.

Investing in your 50+ workforce
is not just an investment in those individuals.

It is an investment in the learning culture
of your entire organization.

The return compounds."

---

[SEGMENT 6 — WHAT INVESTING LOOKS LIKE | ~60 seconds]
[GRAPHIC: "What It Actually Means to Invest"]

"Let me be specific — because 'invest in your workforce'
disappears in a budget meeting if it's not concrete.

It means digital upskilling pathways built for experienced adults.
Not repurposed content from a program designed for college graduates.

It means structured mentorship that captures what experienced workers know
before they walk out the door.

It means performance planning that includes the 50+ workforce
on the same terms as every other talent segment.

And it means measuring what you've been ignoring —
retention rates, productivity impact, knowledge transfer outcomes.

The data is there if you build the systems to capture it."

---

[SEGMENT 7 — THE COMPETITIVE ADVANTAGE | ~45 seconds]
[GRAPHIC: "2030: 1 in 5 Americans Over 65"]

"By 2030, one in five Americans will be over 65.

The organizations ahead of this shift
stopped treating their 50+ workforce as a liability
and started treating it as an asset to be developed.

The retention rates are higher.
The productivity metrics are stronger.
The mentorship infrastructure is more robust.

Your 50+ workforce is not a problem to be solved.

It is an advantage to be activated.

And the organizations that figure that out first
will have a talent strategy their competitors
cannot replicate quickly.

You cannot manufacture experience.
You can only invest in the people who already have it."

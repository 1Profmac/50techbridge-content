Alright. Let's lay it out. The Under-One-Hundred-Dollar Starter Stack. Pen and paper time. Or open your Notes app. Whatever generation you are.

Tool number one. Your AI assistant.

Claude from Anthropic. The free tier gives you access to one of the most capable AI systems on the planet. You can use it for brainstorming, writing, research, strategy, and problem-solving. If you want more capacity and access to advanced features, the Pro plan is twenty dollars a month.

My recommendation: start free. Upgrade when you're making money. Zero to twenty dollars a month.

[PAUSE]

Tool number two. Your website.

WordPress.com, business plan, twelve dollars a month. This gets you a custom domain, professional themes, and enough functionality to run a legitimate consulting business. You don't need Squarespace. You don't need Wix. You don't need a custom-built site. You need a clean, professional online presence that tells people who you are, what you do, and how to hire you.

Twelve dollars a month.

[PAUSE]

Tool number three. Your email list.

Mailchimp free tier. Five hundred contacts. One thousand emails per month. This is your most valuable asset after your expertise. Social media platforms can change their algorithm tomorrow and your reach goes to zero. Your email list belongs to you. Start building it from day one.

Zero dollars a month.

[PAUSE]

Tool number four. Your social media presence.

LinkedIn. Free. Full stop. This is where your clients are, especially if you're in professional services, consulting, B2B, nonprofit work, or any knowledge-based field. You don't need to be on Instagram. You don't need to make TikToks. You need to be on LinkedIn, posting valuable content consistently.

Zero dollars a month.

[PAUSE]

Tool number five. Your payment system.

Stripe. Free to create an account. Free to set up. They take 2.9% plus thirty cents per transaction, but only when you get paid. That means it costs you nothing until money is coming in. You can send professional invoices, accept credit cards, and set up recurring billing for retainer clients.

Zero dollars a month until you're generating revenue. And then it's a tiny percentage of money you wouldn't have had otherwise.

[PAUSE]

Let's add it up.

If you start with all free tiers: thirty-two dollars a month. That's twelve for WordPress and twenty if you choose Claude Pro.

If you start with the absolute minimum — Claude free tier and WordPress: twelve dollars a month.

The full recommended stack with Claude Pro: fifty-two dollars a month if you add a few extras along the way.

[PAUSE]

Under one hundred dollars. Under fifty dollars for most people. Under fifteen dollars if you're really bootstrapping.

[PAUSE]

Now let me talk about the other side of the money conversation. Not what you spend. What you charge.

This is where people over fifty make their biggest mistake. They underprice themselves. They think, "Well, I'm just starting out as a consultant, so I should charge less."

No. Stop. You are not just starting out. You're just starting out as a business owner. You have been an expert for decades.

[PAUSE]

Here's a framework. Take what your hourly rate was at your last job — your full compensation including benefits, divided by two thousand hours. If you were making a hundred thousand dollars a year, your effective hourly rate was about fifty dollars.

Now double it. Your consulting rate should start at a hundred dollars an hour minimum. Why double? Because you're now covering your own taxes, your own benefits, and your own business costs. And because you're delivering concentrated expertise, not filling a seat for forty hours a week.

[PAUSE]

If you're in a specialized field — cybersecurity, regulatory compliance, financial planning, healthcare administration — your rate should be one-fifty to three hundred an hour. Or better yet, don't charge by the hour at all. Charge by the project. Charge by the outcome. A project audit that takes you twelve hours but saves a nonprofit fifty thousand dollars in wasted resources is worth five thousand dollars, not six hundred.

Price for value. Not for time.

Pacing notes: [PAUSE] = 1 second. [LONG PAUSE] = 2 seconds. [BEAT] = half second breath.

===================================================================
SECTION 1 — THE INTERVIEWER (TWO VERSIONS)
===================================================================
The interviewer appears TWICE in the film — once cold (Episode 1) and once warm (Episode 3).
These are two different emotional registers. Generate as two separate audio files.
Both are OFF-CAMERA. Camera stays on Carol (Ep1) and Marcus (Ep3).

-------------------------------------------------------------------
INTERVIEWER VERSION A — EPISODE 1 (COLD)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Scene: Carol's algorithm interview. Carol has just said:
"I've built three teams from the ground up. I know what you're looking for."
The interviewer responds — but is still looking at the screen, not at her.

Voice style: Corporate. Practiced. Pleasant on the surface.
Not rude — worse than rude. Polite in a way that has already decided.
ElevenLabs: White male, early 30s. Clear, professional, slightly fast.
Medium-high pitch. The voice of someone who thinks he is being helpful.

SCRIPT — INTERVIEWER EP1 (7+ sec / ~22 spoken words):
We appreciate your interest, Carol.

[BEAT]

We're really looking for someone who can grow with us long-term.

[PAUSE]

This role requires a lot of digital fluency —

[BEAT]

day one, from the ground up.

[PAUSE]

We'll be in touch.

---
Output filename: interviewer-cold-ep1.mp3
Camera: Stays on Carol's face the entire time.
She hears "grow with us long-term" and "day one from the ground up."
She knows what those words mean. Her expression does not change.
That stillness is the performance. The interviewer's voice is just the evidence.
Runtime estimate: 8 seconds spoken + 2 seconds pauses = ~10 seconds total

-------------------------------------------------------------------
INTERVIEWER VERSION B — EPISODE 3 (WARM)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Scene: Marcus's comeback interview. Everything is reversed.
Marcus has just said: "I've seen that system fail three times.
I know exactly where it breaks and why. Want me to show you?"
The interviewer responds — this time leaning forward, genuinely engaged.

Voice style: Still professional — but present. Listening. Surprised in a good way.
ElevenLabs: Same voice preset as Version A if possible — same person, different energy.
Or a different voice — either works. What matters is the contrast with Version A.

SCRIPT — INTERVIEWER EP3 (7+ sec / ~20 spoken words):
Walk me through that.

[PAUSE]

The 2019 failure — what specifically did you see that others missed?

[LONG PAUSE — interviewer is actually listening now]

And you documented this at the time?

[BEAT]

I want to hear all of it.

---
Output filename: interviewer-warm-ep3.mp3
Camera: On Marcus's face as he answers — calm, no hesitation.
Then cut to the interviewer leaning forward, nodding.
This 10-second exchange carries the entire arc of the film.
The contrast with Version A is everything — same room, different world.
Runtime estimate: 8 seconds spoken + 3 seconds pauses = ~11 seconds total

===================================================================
SECTION 2 — THE BOARDROOM CONVERSATION
===================================================================
This is a three-person exchange. Tom Briggs presents. Two other executives respond.
It sounds like a real meeting — not a monologue, a decision being made.

Three voices needed. All OFF-CAMERA or shown from behind/side — no lip sync.
Camera cuts between: the slide, executive faces from behind, Tom at the front.

Characters in this scene:
  — TOM BRIGGS (already scripted — use tom-briggs-boardroom.mp3 as the opener)
  — EXECUTIVE A (younger male, 30s — asks the ROI question)
  — EXECUTIVE B (female, 40s — closes the decision)

-------------------------------------------------------------------
EXECUTIVE A — THE ROI QUESTION
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Voice style: Sharp. Data-focused. Not skeptical of the decision — skeptical of
whether the numbers are big enough. He wants more, not less.
ElevenLabs: White male, early 30s. Fast, confident, analytical.

SCRIPT — EXECUTIVE A (7+ sec / ~19 spoken words):
What's the ROI differential between the segments?

[PAUSE]

If we're pulling budget from 50-plus outreach —

[BEAT]

I want to see the acquisition cost comparison before we lock this.

[PAUSE]

How far apart are we talking?

---
Output filename: boardroom-exec-a.mp3
Camera: On the TARGET DEMOGRAPHIC slide as Executive A speaks.
His question sounds reasonable. It is reasonable. That is the point.
Nobody in this room is asking the wrong question by their own logic.
Runtime estimate: 8 seconds spoken + 2 seconds pauses = ~10 seconds total

-------------------------------------------------------------------
TOM BRIGGS — THE ANSWER (EXPANDED VERSION)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
This follows Executive A's question. Tom answers — still no doubt, just data.
Generate as a SECOND Tom Briggs file. Combine with tom-briggs-boardroom.mp3 in editing.

Voice style: Same as tom-briggs-boardroom.mp3 — confident, practiced, boardroom fast.

SCRIPT — TOM BRIGGS ANSWER (7+ sec / ~21 spoken words):
Roughly three to one on customer acquisition cost.

[BEAT]

The 25-to-40 segment converts faster, spends more per transaction,

[BEAT]

and has significantly higher digital adoption out of the gate.

[PAUSE]

The numbers are clear.

---
Output filename: tom-briggs-answer.mp3
Camera: On the slide updating — new charts appearing showing the cost comparison.
Numbers fill the screen. Clean, designed, completely rational.
Runtime estimate: 8 seconds spoken + 1 second beats = ~9 seconds total

-------------------------------------------------------------------
EXECUTIVE B — THE DECISION
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Voice style: Final. Clean. The voice of someone who closes meetings.
Not excited — just efficient. This is the most dangerous line in the film
because it sounds completely normal.
ElevenLabs: Female, early 40s. Crisp, neutral, authoritative.
No emotion in either direction. She is not cruel. She is just done.

SCRIPT — EXECUTIVE B (7+ sec / ~18 spoken words):
The data supports it.

[PAUSE]

Let's move forward with the 25-to-40 roadmap.

[BEAT]

Pull the 50-plus line items from next quarter's budget.

[PAUSE]

We'll revisit if the segment shows traction.

---
Output filename: boardroom-exec-b-decision.mp3
Camera: Slow pan across the executives at the table — all nodding, some typing.
Nobody objects. Nobody raises a hand.
"We'll revisit if the segment shows traction" is the last line before the film cuts
to Marcus at the kiosk. The audience makes the connection without being told.
Runtime estimate: 8 seconds spoken + 2 seconds pauses = ~10 seconds total

===================================================================
VOICE SELECTION GUIDE — NEW CHARACTERS
===================================================================

Interviewer (both versions):
  Use the SAME voice preset for both Ep1 and Ep3 if possible.
  Same person — different energy. The contrast hits harder.
  Try: "Daniel" or "Josh" — professional male, clear, believable.
  Version A: Set expressiveness LOW, stability HIGH. Polite autopilot.
  Version B: Set expressiveness MEDIUM, stability MEDIUM. Actually present.

Executive A:
  Young, fast, data-brain. Try "Charlie" or "Callum."
  He should sound like he just ran the numbers on his laptop 10 minutes ago.

Executive B:
  Clear, decisive, no warmth but no cruelty. Try "Rachel" or "Emily."
  The goal is: completely normal corporate voice making a catastrophic decision.
  She should not sound like a villain. That's what makes her terrifying.

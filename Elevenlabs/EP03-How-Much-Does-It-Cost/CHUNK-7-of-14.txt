I want to tell you a story.

Now, this is a composite. I'm combining elements from several people in our community to protect privacy, but every detail I'm about to share is real. These are real numbers, real timelines, and real outcomes.

Let's call her Sandra.

Sandra is fifty-five years old. Spent twenty-two years in project management. PMP certified. Led teams of fifteen to forty people. Managed budgets in the millions. She worked for two Fortune five hundred companies and a mid-size government contractor.

In March of twenty twenty-five, Sandra got laid off. Restructuring. The company was "going in a different direction," which is corporate-speak for "we're replacing your salary with software licenses."

Now Sandra did what most people do. She updated her resume. She went on LinkedIn. She applied for jobs. And she ran straight into the wall that a lot of people over fifty run into: she was either overqualified, too expensive, or both. She'd get interviews, make it to the second round, and then hear nothing. You know the silence. That silence is loud.

Sandra started thinking about consulting. She'd heard people talk about it. "You should start your own thing." Easy to say when you're the one saying it and not the one doing it.

Her first thought? "I'd need to save up. Get a business loan. Probably ten, fifteen thousand dollars to get set up properly."

She actually went to a SCORE workshop, SCORE is the SBA's mentoring program, great organization, and the mentor told her, "You could be up and running for a few hundred dollars."

Sandra did not believe him.

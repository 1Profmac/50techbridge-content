Social media. Sandra already had a LinkedIn profile, but it read like a resume. She used Claude to rewrite it as a consulting profile. She started posting twice a week. Not viral content. Not dancing on camera. Practical, useful insights about project management for nonprofits. Things like "three signs your nonprofit has outgrown its project management process."

Cost for LinkedIn? Zero. Time investment? About two hours a week. And Claude helped her draft every post.

Email marketing. She signed up for Mailchimp's free tier. That gives you up to five hundred contacts and one thousand emails per month. More than enough for the first six months. She created a simple lead magnet, a one-page PDF called "The Nonprofit Project Health Checklist," again, written with Claude's help. She put it on her website. People started downloading it. Not a flood. A trickle. But a trickle of exactly the right people.

Now here's where it gets good.

Month two. Sandra posted an article on LinkedIn about the three biggest project management mistakes she saw in nonprofits. It got eleven hundred views. Not viral. But one of those eleven hundred viewers was the executive director of a mid-size nonprofit in her city.

That executive director sent her a message: "This is exactly what we're dealing with. Can we talk?"

Sandra had a thirty-minute call. She listened more than she talked. She asked questions that only someone with twenty-two years of experience would think to ask. And at the end of the call, she proposed a project audit. Two weeks. Twenty-five hundred dollars.

The executive director said yes the next day.

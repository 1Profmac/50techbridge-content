## SECTION 4: PROJECT DESCRIPTION

### 4.1 The Program

The **Digital Pioneer Gallery** is a structured, hybrid (in-person + online) digital arts training and public exhibition program exclusively for adults 50 and older in Austin, TX.

The program runs in cohort cycles:

| Phase | Duration | Activities |
|---|---|---|
| **Training** | 8 weeks | Bi-weekly in-person sessions + live online support. Canva (digital design), MailerLite (email audience), Wave (audio/visual editing), digital photography basics |
| **Portfolio Development** | 3 weeks | 1:1 review, digital portfolio build, print-ready artwork preparation |
| **Public Exhibition** | 1 event per cohort | Gallery opening at Carver Museum, Austin Central Library, or partnered hotel/venue — open to residents and visitors |

**Annual output: 6 cohort cycles → 6 public gallery exhibitions**

Each cohort: 12–15 participants  
Annual participants served: 72–90 adults 50+  
Annual gallery openings: 6 public events

### 4.2 Why This Program, Why Now

Austin's creative funding ecosystem is structurally youth-centric. SXSW, ACL, and Austin's cultural identity skew toward artists under 35. Adults 50+ — who built this city, whose stories are Austin's cultural history — are excluded from gallery systems, digital training pipelines, and public arts recognition.

**The Digital Pioneer Gallery is the correction.**

50+TechBridge has already built the training infrastructure, the library partnerships, the trust, and the participant pipeline. The Digital Pioneer Gallery adds the destination: a public gallery wall where participants' digital artwork is exhibited, celebrated, and sold.

### 4.3 The Pipeline

```
Creative interest → Digital skills training (8 weeks)
→ Portfolio development (3 weeks) → Public exhibition (gallery opening)
→ Alumni community → Income opportunities (art sales, commissions)
```

This pipeline did not exist in Austin for adults 50+ before this program. It now exists. ACME Elevate funding enables us to scale it to 6 public exhibitions per year and serve 72–90 participants annually.

## SECTION 5: ALIGNMENT WITH ACME ELEVATE CRITERIA

### 5.1 Historically Underrepresented Communities in Funding

ACME's own eligibility language requires applicants to support "historically underrepresented communities in funding." Adults 50+ are exactly that population.

Austin's arts funding ecosystem is documented to skew toward emerging artists under 35. The Digital Pioneer Gallery serves the demographic most systematically excluded from gallery representation, digital arts training, and public cultural recognition.

Our participant base reflects Austin's underrepresented 50+ communities:

| Group | Est. Adults 50+ in Austin | Digital Pioneer Gallery Reach |
|---|---|---|
| Hispanic / Latino | ~64,000 | Primary recruitment: East Austin + South Austin libraries |
| Black / African American | ~14,900 | Primary recruitment: Carver Museum + East Austin libraries |
| Combined BIPOC 50+ | ~107,000 | Majority of all cohorts |

Total adults 50+ in Austin: ~198,000. Total BIPOC adults 50+: ~107,000 (54%). The grant alignment is exact.

### 5.2 Elevating Underrepresented Histories and Emerging Cultural Forms

The lived histories of Austin's 50+ Black and Latino creative community are underrepresented histories by any measure. These adults built East Austin. They raised families in South Austin before it gentrified. Their stories are the city's actual cultural history — not the marketed version.

The **Digital Pioneer Gallery** creates the first structured public pathway for those stories to appear on gallery walls:
- As digital paintings and graphic art created in Canva by 70-year-old first-time designers
- As photographs of Austin neighborhoods taken by longtime residents who learned to shoot and edit digitally through our program
- As visual storytelling by adults who have never had their work displayed publicly before

This is an **emerging cultural form** by definition: hybrid digital/traditional art made by adults 50+, in a city that has never systematically supported this creative population.

### 5.3 Hotel Occupancy Tax Alignment

ACME Elevate is funded by Austin's Hotel Occupancy Tax (HOT). Under Texas law, HOT must support activities that promote tourism and enhance the arts for residents and visitors.

The Digital Pioneer Gallery generates exactly the cultural tourism activity HOT is designed to fund:

- **6 public gallery openings per year** — ticketed cultural events at Carver Museum, Austin Central Library, and hotel/venue partners that are open to residents and out-of-market visitors
- **Authentic Austin storytelling** — the lived histories of longtime Austin residents displayed in venues serving the tourism economy
- **Hotel and travel sector integration** — 50+ artists from Austin's historically underrepresented communities connect directly with hotels, event venues, and travel businesses seeking authentic Austin art, commissions, and cultural programming
- **Recurring cultural asset** — gallery openings function as tourism-draw events that can attract media coverage and out-of-market visitors, supporting the HOT revenue cycle ACME depends on

### 5.4 Digital and Media Arts Focus

Every deliverable in the Digital Pioneer Gallery is a digital or media arts product:
- Digital design (Canva) — graphic art, illustrated portraits, typographic pieces
- Digital photography (iPhone/tablet) — edited using Wave and mobile tools
- Digital video short (optional advanced track) — participant story in 60 seconds
- Digital portfolio (live URL) — each participant exits with a public portfolio page

This is not a traditional visual arts program with a digital component bolted on. It is a **digital arts program** that produces gallery-ready work through the same tools professionals use — sized down to be accessible to first-time creators 50+.

## SECTION 6: COMMUNITY IMPACT

### 6.1 The Technology Gap Among 50+ Adults

AARP's 2026 Technology Trends research (the most comprehensive dataset on 50+ tech adoption in the US) confirms:

- 99% of adults 50+ own at least one technology device — **adoption is not the barrier**
- 90% own smartphones, up from 55% in 2016
- AI usage doubled in one year: 18% in 2024 → 30% in 2025
- Despite high ownership rates, **a sharp gap between adoption and comfort persists**
- The root problem is design mismatch: mainstream creative tools are built for younger users, not for adults with age-related visual, motor, or cognitive differences

50+TechBridge was built to close the adoption/comfort gap. The Digital Pioneer Gallery channels that training toward creative expression and public cultural participation.

### 6.2 The Market Is Large, Underserved, and Uncontested

- ~198,000 adults 50+ in Austin
- ~107,000 are BIPOC (54% of the 50+ population)
- ~40,000+ estimated to have active creative practices (drawing, writing, photography, fiber arts, crafts) with no current path to digital skills training or public exhibition
- **0 other programs** in Austin currently offer a structured, free, hybrid digital arts training and public gallery pipeline specifically for adults 50+

The market is large, demographically underserved, grant-aligned, and entirely uncontested.

### 6.3 Economic Impact for Participants

The Digital Pioneer Gallery is not a recreational program. It is an economic asset for participants:

- Every participant exits with a live digital portfolio (income-generating asset)
- Every participant exits with an active email list (owned audience, not rented platform)
- Gallery openings create opportunities for art sales, print commissions, and corporate art placement
- Hotel and event venue partners provide direct commercial pathways for 50+ artists

Adults on fixed incomes who can generate $200–$1,000 from a gallery sale are materially different after the program than before it.

## SECTION 11: NARRATIVE STATEMENTS

### Why is this project needed now?

Austin is one of the youngest major cities in America (median age 34.7 vs. national 38.9). Adults 50+ are a smaller share of the population and almost entirely absent from Austin's creative funding conversation. They exist. They create. They are not served.

The Digital Pioneer Gallery closes that gap — with a proven organization, proven training model, and proven community trust. ACME Elevate funding transforms what 50+TechBridge has already built into a recurring public arts program.

### What makes this organization qualified?

- 347+ adults trained with a 3x industry average completion rate — not a proposal, a track record
- 9 Austin Public Library partner locations — the institutional relationships are already in place
- MBE-certified — procurement-eligible, equity-aligned, grant-ready
- Alumni network built — Cohort 1 participants will serve as peer mentors in Cohort 3 before the grant year ends
- Founder/instructor (Brian McKinney) brings both workforce development expertise and lived experience as a 50+ entrepreneur who bridges technology and community

### What is the long-term vision?

Year 1 (FY27): 6 gallery exhibitions, 72–90 participants, establish program infrastructure.  
Year 2 (FY28): 8–10 exhibitions, expand to San Antonio and Houston library partners, pursue NEA and Texas Commission on the Arts supplemental funding.  
Year 3 (FY29): Digital Pioneer Gallery becomes a recognized, self-sustaining Austin cultural institution — with a permanent virtual gallery, an annual flagship exhibition, and a national model for 50+ digital arts programs.

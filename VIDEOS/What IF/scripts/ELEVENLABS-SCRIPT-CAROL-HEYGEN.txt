VOICE PROFILE — CAROL SMITH
===================================================================
Age: 54 | White woman | Silver-streaked dark chestnut hair | Navy blazer
Voice style: Composed. Professional. Confident without aggression.
The voice of someone who has done this before and is not afraid of the room.

She is the most qualified person in this interview.
She sounds like it. That is exactly what makes what happens next so devastating.

  Stability:      70

  Style:          25
  Speaker Boost:  ON
  Speed:          Normal — she is measured, not slow

Voice to try: White female, mid-50s, American. "Sarah" / "Laura" / "Charlotte" preset.
Clear consonants. No breathiness. No upward inflection at the end of sentences.
She is not asking if you're impressed. She is telling you what she's done.

Reject any voice that sounds:
  — Young (under 45 feel)
  — Warm or soft
  — Nervous or tentative
  — Like she is asking permission

Keep the voice that sounds like:
  Someone who built three teams and still had to fight for this interview.

===================================================================
CAROL SESSION — EPISODE 1, ALGORITHM INTERVIEW
===================================================================
Context: Carol sits across from the cold interviewer. Portfolio open on the table.
Three questions written inside it that she never gets to ask.
The interviewer has not looked up from the screen.
Carol speaks first — because that is what prepared professionals do.

She does not know there is a screen behind her showing "FLAG: DEMOGRAPHIC RISK."
The audience does. That gap is the entire scene.

After Carol speaks, the camera slowly pushes past her toward the screen.
She remains in foreground, slightly soft. Waiting for a conversation that will not begin.

SCRIPT — ALGORITHM INTERVIEW (7+ sec / ~20 spoken words):
I've built three teams from the ground up.

[PAUSE]

Took the last one from four people to twenty-two in eighteen months.

[BEAT]

I know exactly what this role requires.

[PAUSE]

And I know what you're looking for.

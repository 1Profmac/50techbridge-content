These are audio-only lines. No HeyGen needed.
Camera stays on a screen, environment, or reacting face — NOT the speaker.
Generate each character as a separate ElevenLabs voice. Do NOT use Brian's voice ID.

Target: ~7 seconds of spoken content per character.
At natural pace (~2.3 words/sec) that is 15–18 words of actual speech.

Pacing notes: [PAUSE] = 1 second. [LONG PAUSE] = 2 seconds. [BEAT] = half second breath.

===================================================================
CHARACTER 1 — HR SYSTEM VOICE
===================================================================
Voice style: Flat. Robotic. Genderless. No warmth. No hesitation.
ElevenLabs setting: Use "Narrator" preset or lowest warmth setting available.
Speed: Slightly faster than natural speech — automated systems don't pause for you.

SCRIPT (7 sec / ~17 spoken words):
Marcus Johnson.

[PAUSE]

Workforce optimization complete.

[BEAT]

Your position has been eliminated.

[BEAT]

Effective thirty days from today.

[PAUSE]

Please report to Human Resources.

---

Used in: Episode 1, Scene 3 — plays over close-up of the HR dashboard screen
Camera: On the screen. The CONFIRM button. Not on Marcus.

===================================================================
CHARACTER 2 — TOM BRIGGS
===================================================================
Voice style: Corporate presentation. Practiced. Confident. Fast.
The voice of a man who has given this slide 40 times and never questioned it.
ElevenLabs setting: White male, 50s-60s, professional. Medium-low pitch. No gravel.
Speed: Slightly brisk — this is a boardroom presentation, not a conversation.

SCRIPT (7 sec / ~18 spoken words):
The product roadmap targets 25 to 40.

[BEAT]

That's our primary growth segment.

[BEAT]

The 50-plus demographic shows lower adoption rates.

[BEAT]

We've adjusted projections accordingly.

NOT on Tom's face. His voice fills the room but we never see him say it.
Runtime estimate: 7 seconds spoken + 1 second beats = ~8 seconds total

===================================================================
CHARACTER 3 — ROSA GUTIERREZ
===================================================================
Voice style: Polite. Soft. Clear. Not angry — the specific tone of someone
asking for help they know they shouldn't have to ask for.
ElevenLabs setting: Latina female, early 50s. Warm but restrained. Light accent optional.
Speed: Normal — she is being careful to be understood.

SCRIPT (7 sec / ~17 spoken words):
Excuse me —

[PAUSE]

I'm sorry to bother you.

[BEAT]

Could someone help me with this?

[PAUSE]

I can't seem to get it to respond.

---

Camera: On the kiosk screen, then pulls back to show Rosa's hands lowering.
No one answers. That silence is the scene. Do not fill it.
Runtime estimate: 7 seconds spoken + 2 seconds pause = ~9 seconds total

===================================================================
CHARACTER 4 — JAMES OKAFOR
===================================================================
Voice style: Low. Flat. Barely above a murmur.
The voice of a man who has stopped being surprised by bad news.
He is not making a scene. He is never making a scene.
ElevenLabs setting: Black male, late 50s. Deep, quiet, controlled.
Different from Marcus — Marcus has warmth underneath. James has gone quiet.
Speed: Slow. Measured. Each phrase lands before the next one starts.

SCRIPT (7+ sec / ~22 spoken words):
[after Marcus says "That's our department" — 1 second gap before James responds]

I know.

[PAUSE]

I saw the numbers last quarter.

[BEAT]

Three months I've been watching those charts.

[PAUSE]

I've been counting down to this meeting.

---
Output filename: james-okafor-threeroom.mp3
Used in: Episode 1, Scene 6 — Three Workers
Camera: On the optimization screen, the headcount number ticking down.
Timeline note: Marcus line plays first, then 1 second gap, then James responds.
Sync Marcus and James as two separate audio tracks in Canva timeline.
Runtime estimate: 9 seconds spoken + 2 seconds pauses = ~11 seconds total
===================================================================


===================================================================
CHARACTER 5 — DOROTHY WILLIAMS
===================================================================
Voice style: Warm. Surprised. Real. Unfinished sentences are fine.
This is not a performed line — it is a reaction that becomes words.
She is not talking to anyone. She doesn't know she said it out loud.
ElevenLabs setting: Black female, mid-60s. Warm, slightly breathless.
Speed: Natural — the first "oh" comes out before she decides to speak.

SCRIPT (7 sec / ~17 spoken words):
Oh —

[PAUSE]

It actually understood that.

[BEAT]

All of it.

[PAUSE]

I didn't think it would get what I was asking.

[soft breath — leave half second of space after the last word]

---
Output filename: dorothy-realsmile.mp3
Used in: Episode 2, The Real Smile — plays while camera holds on Dorothy's face
Camera: Close-up on her face. Laptop glow warm.
The smile comes AFTER the last line — not during the "oh."
"Oh" is surprise. The smile is recognition. Do not let them overlap.
Runtime estimate: 7 seconds spoken + 2 seconds pauses = ~9 seconds total

===================================================================
ELEVENLABS VOICE SELECTION GUIDE
===================================================================

When picking voices in ElevenLabs for each character, listen for:

HR System:    Flat, even, no inflection. "Narrator" preset or Rachel at
              0 stability, 0 similarity. Should sound like a phone tree.

Tom Briggs:   "Adam" or "Charlie" — professional male, zero personality.
              The voice of someone reading a PowerPoint he has never questioned.

Rosa:         "Bella" or any warm female voice at medium stability.
              The softness matters more than accent. She is not complaining.
              She is asking. Politely. For the third time.

James:        "Arnold" or deep male voice at HIGH stability setting.
              Turn expressiveness all the way down. He has used it all up.
              Generate twice — pick the flatter take.

Dorothy:      "Elli" or "Grace" — warm, natural, slightly older.
              Medium stability, medium similarity. Let it breathe.
              The "oh" should sound like it surprised even her.

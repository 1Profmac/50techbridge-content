## What It Used to Take to Run an Online Business

Not long ago, running an online business required a team. Not because the work was complex — because the work was repetitive. Every day, the same tasks consumed the same hours, and each one required a person or a contractor to handle it.

| Task | Who You Hired | Monthly Cost | Hours/Week |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email management and follow-up | Virtual assistant | $800–$2,000 | 10–15 hrs |
| Social media (create, schedule, respond) | Social media manager | $1,500–$3,000 | 10–20 hrs |
| Bookkeeping and tax prep | Bookkeeper | $300–$500 | 5–10 hrs/mo |
| Customer service (inbox, questions, support) | Support rep | $1,500–$2,500 | 15–20 hrs |
| Content writing (blog posts, emails, copy) | Copywriter | $400–$2,000 | 8–12 hrs |
| Website updates and maintenance | Webmaster | $200–$500 | 3–5 hrs |
| Marketing and ads | Marketing consultant | $1,000–$3,000 | 5–10 hrs |
| Scheduling and calendar management | Admin assistant | $800–$2,000 | 4–5 hrs |
| Reports and data entry | Admin assistant | (included above) | 3–5 hrs |
| Legal (contracts, terms) | Lawyer on retainer | $400–$1,250 | As needed |
| **TOTAL** | **5–7 people** | **$7,000–$16,750/mo** | **60–100 hrs/wk** |

That is $84,000 to $201,000 a year — before you sell a single product.

For most adults 50+ with a business idea and decades of expertise, that number killed the dream before it started.

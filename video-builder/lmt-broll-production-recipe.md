# THE 6-PHASE RECIPE

## Phase 1 — Pre-production (15 min)

### The 3-bullet script template
Every LMT short video uses this structure:

```
HOOK (5 sec)         — One sentence that names the problem
SETUP (15 sec)       — Why this matters now
BULLET 1 (15 sec)    — Stat + visual + Brian voice
BULLET 2 (15 sec)    — Stat + visual + Brian voice
BULLET 3 (15 sec)    — Stat + visual + Brian voice
RESOLUTION (15 sec)  — What to do about it
CTA (10 sec)         — Where to go next
```

Total: ~90 seconds. Trim to 75 if needed.

### B-Roll shot list (what to look for, every video)
For each bullet, you need ONE 5–8 second b-roll clip showing:
- A workplace scene (office, hospital, factory, retail, etc.)
- People aged 50+ doing professional work, NOT being rescued
- Multigenerational interaction is bonus
- Avoid: stock smiles, fake cheers, computer-screen close-ups, anything sterile or staged

### Stat sourcing checklist
Every stat needs:
- Source name (AARP, BLS, LSE, Protiviti, your own data)
- Year
- A URL you can paste in the description
- A one-sentence translation in Brian's voice

### Avatar settings (locked in for every LMT video)
- **Avatar:** Brian's "straight talk" avatar (the direct-gaze one)
- **Background:** Either pure green `#00FF00` (for chromakey pipeline) OR navy `#0E1C2F` (for simple overlay) — pick ONE per video and stick with it

## Phase 4 — Canva assembly with AARP-style motion (45 min) ⭐

This is the phase you specifically asked about — the **pan in/out, scene changes, and Ken Burns techniques** that make AARP videos feel premium.

### The 6 motion techniques you need to master

#### Technique 1 — The Slow Zoom (Ken Burns effect)
**What it does:** Adds slow continuous motion to a static or low-motion clip so the viewer's eye stays engaged. AARP uses this on every b-roll clip.

**How in Canva:**
1. Click on your video clip
2. Top toolbar → **Animate**
3. Select **Pan** (this is Canva's name for Ken Burns motion)
4. Choose **Pan Right** or **Pan Down** (slowest, most cinematic)
5. Duration: leave at default (matches the clip length)

**LMT rule:** Use Pan on EVERY b-roll clip. Never let a clip sit still for more than 2 seconds without motion.

**Pro variant — manual zoom in:**
1. Drop the same b-roll clip on TWO consecutive pages
2. Page 1: clip at 100% size (full frame)
3. Page 2: clip at 110% size (zoomed in 10%)
4. Set page transition between them to **Dissolve**
5. Effect: the shot subtly punches in across the cut

#### Technique 2 — The Slow Pan (left-to-right or right-to-left)
**What it does:** Reveals more of the scene over time. Used for wide shots like "factory floor" or "boardroom."

**How in Canva:**
1. Make the video slightly larger than the canvas (e.g., 2200×1238 on a 1920×1080 page)
2. Position it with the LEFT edge inside the canvas, RIGHT edge cropped off
3. Animate → **Pan Right**
4. Canva will slowly slide the clip left to right across the duration

**Manual pan workaround if Animate doesn't give you the direction you want:**
1. Page 1: position the video with focal point on the LEFT
2. Page 2: position the video with focal point on the RIGHT
3. Page transition: **Slide** (set direction to right)
4. The two pages stitch into one continuous pan

#### Technique 3 — Punch-In on Stats
**What it does:** When a big number appears, the b-roll clip behind it punches in 5–10% so the eye shifts to the stat.

**How in Canva:**
1. Page A: b-roll clip at 100%, no text
2. Page B: same clip at 108–110%, with the stat text overlay
3. Page transition: **Dissolve** (1 second)
4. Animate the stat text: **Bounce In** at 0:00 of Page B
5. Effect: visual punch-in + text snap = AARP-style emphasis

#### Technique 4 — Scene Change Cuts (the rhythm of AARP)
**What it does:** Cuts between b-roll clips every 4–8 seconds. Fast enough to keep attention, slow enough to register the visual.

**The AARP rhythm:** Watch any AARP news video and count the cuts. You'll find ~12–16 cuts in a 2-minute video. That's a cut every 7–10 seconds on average, with some bursts of 3–4 second cuts during stat moments.

**How in Canva:**
- Each new b-roll clip = a new page
- Page transition: **Dissolve** (default, 0.5s) for most cuts
- For a "punch" cut between two related shots, use **None** (hard cut, no transition)
- For a "narrative beat" cut (going from one section to another), use **Fade to Black** (0.8s)

**The 3 transition types and when to use them:**

| Transition | When | Speed | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dissolve** | Default — most scene changes | 0.5s | B-roll clip A → B-roll clip B |
| **None (hard cut)** | High-energy, related shots | 0s | Wide shot → close-up of same scene |
| **Fade to Black** | Section breaks, dramatic emphasis | 0.8s | End of Bullet 1 → start of Bullet 2 |
| **Slide** | Reveals or "next chapter" feel | 0.6s | Brian on screen → text card |

**LMT rule:** 60% Dissolve, 25% Hard Cut, 10% Fade to Black, 5% Slide. Mix them.

#### Technique 5 — The L-Cut (audio leads)
**What it does:** Brian's voice starts talking BEFORE you see his face. You hear him over a b-roll clip, then the next page reveals him on camera.

**How in Canva:**
- Page A (b-roll only, 6 seconds): visible scene is a workplace, but the audio playing is Brian's voice from his next page
- Page B (Brian on camera, 6 seconds): same audio continues
- Effect: smooth narrative flow, no abrupt voice-on-face landing

**Canva limitation:** Canva doesn't support true L-cuts in the basic editor. **Workaround:**
- Render Brian's voice as a separate audio file (export from HeyGen as MP3)
- In Canva: Audio tab → upload the MP3 → drag onto the timeline → it plays continuously across page changes
- Brian's video on the camera-on pages is muted (right-click video → Mute)
- Result: voice plays continuously over both b-roll and on-camera pages

#### Technique 6 — The Hold (the most under-used technique)
**What it does:** A long static shot — 5+ seconds without motion — at a key emotional moment. The opposite of all the pan/zoom/cut activity.

**When to use:**
- The closing line ("The room is open.")
- The hardest stat ("$850 billion lost every year.")
- Brian's most direct sentence ("I built this because...")

**How in Canva:**
- Page duration: 6–8 seconds
- No animation on the video element
- Text fades in and stays
- No page transition out (cut hard to the next page)

**LMT rule:** Use a Hold once per video. Never twice. Save it for the moment that matters most.

### The standard LMT page architecture

Every page has up to 6 layers stacked from bottom to top:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  LAYER 6 — Lower-third name bar          │  ← bottom of frame, only on Brian-on-camera pages
├─────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  LAYER 5 — Brand bug (LMT logo)          │  ← top-left, ALWAYS visible
├─────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  LAYER 4 — Text overlay (stat or quote)  │  ← center, fades in/out
├─────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  LAYER 3 — Brian PIP video (320x180)     │  ← bottom-right, on b-roll pages
├─────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  LAYER 2 — B-roll video (full frame)     │  ← Pan/zoom animation
├─────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  LAYER 1 — Navy background #0E1C2F       │  ← always
└─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Build pages in this order: navy → b-roll → Brian PIP → text → brand bug → lower-third.

---

### Animation timing rules (memorize these)

| Element | Animation | Duration | When it triggers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Text headline | Fade In | 0.6s | 0:00 of page |
| Text subhead | Fade In | 0.6s | 0:01 of page (delayed 1s) |
| Big stat number | Bounce In (subtle) | 0.5s | 0:00 |
| B-roll clip | Pan (Animate menu) | full clip length | continuous |
| Brian PIP | Fade In | 0.4s | 0:00 |
| Lower-third bar | Slide Up | 0.6s | 0:00 |
| Brand bug | None — always visible | n/a | never animates |
| Page transition | Dissolve | 0.5s | between pages |

## Phase 5 — Audio (5 min)

- **Brian's voice:** baked into HeyGen render OR exported as separate MP3 (for L-cut technique)
- **Background music:** optional, 25% volume only, NEVER over the first or last 5 seconds (let his voice carry the open and close)
- **Recommended music style:** ambient cinematic, instrumental piano, no vocals
- **Where to find:** Canva Audio tab → search "ambient cinematic" / "corporate inspirational" / "minimal piano"

# THE "AARP LOOK" ESSENCE (one paragraph)

The AARP visual signature is **continuous slow motion on every b-roll clip + a cut every 4–8 seconds + one big stat punching in per bullet + a name lower-third under the host + one Hold at the key moment + clean fade dissolves between scenes**. Master those 6 things and your videos will look like AARP made them — without the AARP budget. Canva Pro gives you all 6 techniques natively. The recipe is repeatable. Build it once, repeat it forever.

The Data Behind Our Approach

A 50-year-old founder is 2.8 times more likely to succeed than a 25-year-old (MIT, 2.7 million founders studied). 52.3% of all U.S. businesses are owned by people 55 and older (U.S. Census Bureau). RAND Corporation found that digital literacy training tripled employment rates from 14% to 40% within two months. One digital skill boosts earnings by 23%. Three or more raise wages by 45%.

Yet since 2000, workforce training funding has been cut in half, adjusted for inflation. Adults over 50 are systematically underserved by existing programs. 22 million older adults lack broadband access at home. The gap between what the data proves and what organizations invest is the $850 billion problem.

## 2. ETPL Requirements for Texas

### What Is the ETPL?

The Statewide Eligible Training Providers List (ETPL) is the list of programs TWC has approved for Individual Training Account (ITA) funding under WIOA. Being on the ETPL does NOT guarantee referrals or funding — it makes LMT eligible to receive ITA payments when WIOA participants choose LMT's programs.

### Eligibility Requirements

To get on the Texas ETPL independently, a training provider must meet ALL of the following:

1. **Program Alignment:** Programs must prepare students for occupations on the statewide Target Occupations List. Digital skills/AI training likely qualifies if mapped to occupations like Computer User Support Specialists (SOC 15-1232), Computer and Information Systems Managers, or similar in-demand IT occupations.

2. **Licensing/Accreditation:** Provider must be appropriately licensed, regulated, or accredited under Texas Education Code Chapter 62 AND meet requirements or exemption criteria under Chapter 132 (Career Schools and Colleges Act). This is the biggest hurdle for an LLC.

3. **Business Partnerships:** Must show proof of partnerships with local businesses for the program. LMT's 23 partner organizations are a major asset here.

4. **Performance Data:** Must provide verifiable student-level performance data (Student Data Reports/SDRs) for ALL students in the program. New programs must submit SDRs before appearing on the statewide ETPL.

5. **Performance Standards (2025-2027 cycle):**
   - 70% employment rate in Q2 post-exit
   - 70% employment rate in Q4 post-exit
   - Median earnings of $7,800
   - 50% credential rate
   - 60% completion rate (LMT's 3X industry completion rate is a standout here)

### Application Process (7 Steps)

1. Provider submits provider, campus, and program information to the local Workforce Development Board (Workforce Solutions Capital Area for Travis County)
2. Board reviews and may request updates
3. Board submits provider/program information to TWC (pending student data)
4. TWC reviews and may request modifications
5. Provider submits completed Student Data Report (SDR)
6. TWC conducts final review
7. Programs are added to statewide ETPL

**Timeline:** 2-6 weeks AFTER all prerequisites (licensing/exemption) are in place.

### Key Limitation

An LLC cannot simply apply to ETPL without first having either:
- A TWC Career School Certificate of Approval, OR
- A TWC Letter of Exemption under Chapter 132, OR
- Being an institution of higher education, OR
- Being a registered apprenticeship sponsor

## 3. TWC Career School Certification & Exemptions

### Option A: Full Career School Certificate of Approval

**What it is:** TWC license required for any business providing instruction in Texas. If you run a business that provides instruction in Texas or to Texans, you need a license — including distance education providers.

**Timeline:** TWC aims for 90 calendar days. Review cycles of 5 business days, with 30-day response windows. Can take 3+ review cycles.

**Cost:** Minimum $1,131 for a small school (1 director, 1 instructor, 1 representative). Fees scale with staff size.

**Requirements:**
- Complete application with supporting documents
- Facility inspection (if applicable)
- Curriculum review
- Qualified instructors
- Financial stability documentation
- Surety bond or alternative student protection

**Contact:** career.schools@twc.texas.gov | 866-256-6333, option 3

**Verdict for LMT:** This is the heavy path. 3+ months, significant paperwork, ongoing compliance requirements. Only worth pursuing if the subcontractor/partnership paths don't work.

### Option B: Chapter 132 Exemption (RECOMMENDED if going independent)

Texas Education Code Chapter 132 provides several exemption categories. The most relevant for LMT:

#### Exemption Under Section 132.003 — Career Skills Course Exemption

A course of instruction is EXEMPT from Chapter 132 if ALL of the following are true:
- Length is **24 classroom hours or less**
- Fee is **less than $500**
- Provides knowledge/skills to maintain or enhance competency in a business, trade, or occupation
- Does NOT award credits transferable toward longer programs
- Written course description and refund policy provided at least 14 days before start

**This is highly relevant to LMT.** If each of the 6 modules is structured as a standalone course of 24 hours or less at under $500, LMT could qualify for this exemption without full career school licensing.

**Requirements if using this exemption:**
- Must offer refunds to students who complete at least 8 hours (or half the course) and express dissatisfaction within 14 days
- Must maintain 3-year records of student attendance, fees collected, and refunds issued

#### Exemption Under Section 132.004 — Business Enterprise Exemption

A business enterprise that offers EXCLUSIVELY courses or programs exempt under 132.002 or 132.003 is itself exempt from the entire chapter. This means if ALL of LMT's offerings qualify under 132.003, LMT as an entity is exempt.

#### Other Potentially Relevant Exemptions (Section 132.002)

- **Technology vendor training (#15):** Instruction by manufacturers/developers "in the use of technological hardware or software" to purchasers. If LMT licenses its own AI tools, this could apply.
- **Employer-sponsored training (#4):** Courses sponsored by an employer for its own employees at no charge. Relevant if LMT delivers training on behalf of employers.

#### How to Apply for Exemption

Submit formal request with documentation to:

**Texas Workforce Commission**
Career Schools and Colleges
Attn: Unregistered Schools and Exemptions
101 E 15th St, Rm 226-T
Austin, TX 78778-0001

**Timeline:** TWC responds within **45-60 days** whether granted, denied, or more information needed. Denials may be appealed.

### Critical Question: Can an Exempt Provider Get on ETPL?

Yes, but with a caveat. TWC's ETPL requirements state providers must be "appropriately licensed, regulated, or accredited, or must be exempt from regulation under Texas Education Code, Chapter 132, **with authorization in a Letter of Exemption from TWC Career Schools and Colleges.**"

This means:
1. Apply for 132.003/132.004 exemption → receive Letter of Exemption (45-60 days)
2. Use Letter of Exemption to apply for ETPL through Workforce Solutions Capital Area (2-6 weeks)
3. Total timeline: approximately 3-4 months

## 4. Subcontractor Path

### How It Works

An already-approved ETPL provider contracts with LMT to deliver training on their behalf. The WIOA funding flows to the ETPL provider, who pays LMT as a subcontractor. LMT does NOT need to be on the ETPL or hold a career school license.

### Legal Basis

Under 20 CFR 680.320, local workforce boards can use contracts (instead of ITAs) when:

1. **Services are OJT, customized training, incumbent worker training, or transitional jobs** — These bypass ETPL entirely
2. **Insufficient local providers** — Board determines not enough ETPL providers exist for a particular type of training
3. **Community-based organizations with demonstrated effectiveness** — Board identifies CBOs or private organizations with PROVEN training programs for individuals facing employment barriers. Criteria include financial stability, completion rates, placement success, and employment retention. **LMT's 347 completions and 3X completion rate directly support this exception.**
4. **Higher education institution partnership** — Board contracts with a college to train multiple individuals in in-demand sectors
5. **Pay-for-Performance contracts** — Performance-based funding agreements

### Typical Subcontractor Arrangements

**Revenue Split:** Typically 70-85% to the training delivery organization (LMT), 15-30% retained by the ETPL provider for administrative overhead, compliance, reporting, and ITA management. Exact split is negotiated.

**Contract Structure:**
- Master Services Agreement between ETPL provider and LMT
- Scope of work defining curriculum, hours, outcomes
- Performance requirements aligned with WIOA metrics
- Reporting obligations (student data, attendance, outcomes)
- Insurance and liability provisions
- Term typically 1 year with renewal options

**What LMT Provides:**
- Curriculum delivery
- Instructors
- Student support
- Attendance tracking
- Outcome data (employment, credentials)

**What ETPL Provider Provides:**
- WIOA eligibility and enrollment processing
- ITA billing and compliance
- TWC reporting
- Student Data Report submission
- Program listing on ETPL

### Advantages for LMT

- **No licensing requirement** — LMT operates under the ETPL provider's authorization
- **Speed** — Can begin as soon as a contract is signed (days/weeks, not months)
- **Lower risk** — No upfront investment in licensing, bonding, or compliance infrastructure
- **Proof of concept** — Builds a WIOA track record that strengthens a future independent ETPL application

### Disadvantages

- Lower per-student revenue (70-85% vs 100%)
- Less control over enrollment and marketing
- Dependent on partner's compliance and standing
- May not build LMT's independent ETPL eligibility

### Does MBE Certification Provide Shortcuts?

**Not directly for ETPL.** MBE certification does not exempt LMT from licensing or ETPL requirements. However:

- MBE status strengthens any proposal to Workforce Solutions Capital Area (boards have equity goals)
- MBE status qualifies for state HUB procurement advantages
- MBE status is persuasive in grant applications and partnership proposals
- Federal contracts have 23% small business goal, with sub-goals for minority-owned businesses
- City of Austin and Travis County have MBE/WBE participation goals in contracts

### Does Partnering with an Accredited Institution Bypass Licensing?

**Yes.** If LMT delivers training under the umbrella of an accredited institution (e.g., ACC), LMT does not need its own career school license. The institution's accreditation covers the program. LMT would operate as a contract instructor or subcontractor.

### HUB (Historically Underutilized Business) Certification

LMT should obtain Texas HUB certification in addition to existing MBE certification:

**Requirements:**
- 51% owned, managed, and operated by eligible individuals (minority, women, or service-disabled veteran)
- For-profit Texas business
- Existing MBE certification may streamline the process

**How to Apply:**
- Through the City of Austin's MBE/WBE application process, indicate interest in HUB certification
- Austin Small and Minority Business Resources will forward to Texas Comptroller
- Or apply directly through Texas Comptroller
- **Website:** austintexas.gov/department/certification-division

**Benefit:** HUB certification is required/preferred for many state agency procurements including workforce training contracts.

## 7. SAM.gov Registration

### Why It's Needed

SAM.gov registration is required to:
- Receive federal grant funds (including WIOA pass-through)
- Bid on federal contracts
- Be listed in government contractor databases
- Obtain a Unique Entity Identifier (UEI)

### Registration Process

1. **Get a UEI** — Go to SAM.gov, click "Get Started" on "Register Your Entity or Get a Unique Entity ID"
2. **Gather documents:**
   - EIN (must match IRS records EXACTLY — spelling, punctuation, capitalization)
   - Legal business name (must match IRS records exactly)
   - Physical and mailing addresses
   - Banking information for payment processing
   - NAICS codes (likely 611430 - Professional and Management Development Training, and/or 611420 - Computer Training)
   - Government Business POC and Electronic Business POC (can be same person for small business)
3. **Complete registration** — Fill in entity type, business size, fiscal year-end, highway districts
4. **Submit and wait** — Validation through IRS and CAGE code systems

### Timeline

- **Initiation:** 10-15 minutes
- **Completion:** 2-4 weeks typical, up to 6 weeks if issues arise
- **Cost:** FREE (beware of third-party sites that charge fees)
- **Renewal:** Annual (free)

### Common Delays

- Legal name mismatch with IRS records (most common)
- Address validation failure (use exact USPS-formatted address)
- Duplicate UEI issues
- CAGE code linking problems

### Action Item

**Start SAM.gov registration TODAY.** It takes weeks and is required for many funding paths. There is zero downside to having it.

**Website:** sam.gov/entity-registration

## 8. Texas SmartBuy & State Procurement

### CMBL Registration (Centralized Master Bidders List)

**What it is:** The state's master vendor database. When state agencies buy goods/services over $5,000, they must search the CMBL for vendors.

**Why LMT needs it:** Any state agency (including TWC, TEA, HHSC) procurement for training services will search CMBL.

**Registration process:**
1. Go to Texas Comptroller eSystems: comptroller.texas.gov
2. Click "Create Password" to set up eSystems account
3. Select "Apply for CMBL" from eSystems Menu
4. Provide:
   - EIN
   - Legal business name
   - Tax ID
   - Contact details
   - NIGP (National Institute of Governmental Purchasing) commodity codes
   - Company business structure
   - Texas highway district(s) where services are available

**Cost:** $70/year

**Commodity Codes to Select:**
- 952-38: Training, Computer
- 952-44: Training, Employment/Vocational
- 952-71: Training, Technical
- 952-86: Training, Workforce Development
- 918-XX: Computer/IT services codes

**Once registered:** LMT appears in Texas SmartBuy (txsmartbuy.gov), the state's online procurement marketplace. State agencies, local governments, and higher education institutions can find and contract with LMT.

### HUB + CMBL Combination

Registering as both HUB-certified AND on the CMBL is the power combination for state procurement. State agencies have HUB participation goals and actively seek HUB vendors on the CMBL.

## 9. Quickest Path — Ranked Options

### Rank 1: Subcontractor to Existing ETPL Provider (FASTEST — 2-4 weeks)

**Speed:** Can begin delivering training as soon as contract is signed
**Requirements on LMT:** Business license, insurance, contract execution
**Revenue:** 70-85% of WIOA training rate
**Best targets:** ACC Continuing Education, Goodwill Central Texas, Capital IDEA
**Risk:** Low — no compliance burden on LMT
**Limitation:** Dependent on partner; LMT doesn't build independent ETPL standing

### Rank 2: Direct Contract via Workforce Board Exception (4-8 weeks)

**Speed:** Requires board relationship and procurement process
**Legal basis:** 20 CFR 680.320, Exception 3 — community-based organizations with demonstrated effectiveness for individuals facing barriers to employment
**LMT's case is strong:**
- 347 completions (proven track record)
- 3X industry completion rate (demonstrated effectiveness)
- 23 partner organizations (community relationships)
- Serves adults 50+ (documented barrier population)
- MBE-certified (equity alignment)
**Revenue:** 100% of contract value
**Risk:** Medium — requires board buy-in and may take longer than subcontracting
**Action:** Pitch directly to Workforce Solutions Capital Area board/staff

### Rank 3: Partnership with Community College (4-12 weeks)

**Speed:** Depends on ACC's internal approval process for new programs
**How it works:** ACC lists the program on ETPL under their institution. LMT delivers instruction. Revenue is shared or LMT is paid per cohort.
**Revenue:** Varies — typically 60-80% to LMT after ACC overhead
**Risk:** Low-medium — subject to ACC approval process and academic calendar
**Long-term value:** HIGH — builds institutional relationship, scalable, credibility

### Rank 4: Independent ETPL via Chapter 132 Exemption (3-5 months)

**Speed:** 45-60 days for exemption + 2-6 weeks for ETPL application
**Requirements:** Restructure courses to meet 132.003 criteria (≤24 hours, <$500 each)
**Revenue:** 100% of ITA value
**Risk:** Medium — exemption approval is not guaranteed
**Long-term value:** Highest — full independence, direct WIOA billing

### Rank 5: Independent ETPL via Career School Certification (4-7 months)

**Speed:** 90+ days for certification + 2-6 weeks for ETPL application
**Cost:** $1,131+ in fees, plus surety bond, facility requirements
**Revenue:** 100% of ITA value
**Risk:** Higher — significant investment, ongoing compliance burden
**Long-term value:** High — but probably overkill for LMT's current model

### Rank 6: SCSEP Host Agency (Ongoing — not direct WIOA but related)

**Speed:** Depends on AARP Foundation approval process
**How it works:** LMT becomes a host site where SCSEP participants (55+, low-income) receive training. Participants are paid by SCSEP, not LMT.
**Revenue:** Indirect — LMT gets free labor and can charge for premium services
**Value:** Builds pipeline, serves exact target demographic, strengthens WIOA application

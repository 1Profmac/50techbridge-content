## The Question Margaret Asked Me

Margaret is 61 years old.

She took care of her mother for six years
while her mother's health declined —
managing medications, doctor's appointments,
insurance paperwork, and the daily machinery
of keeping another human being alive and cared for.

Her mother passed in 2024.

Margaret came to one of our Lunch & Learn sessions
at a senior center in East Austin about three weeks later.
She sat in the back, quiet, with her hands folded
on the table, and listened to everything I said.

After the session, she found me by the coffee.

She said: "I've been doing this my whole life —
taking care of other people, managing complicated
situations, solving problems nobody else wanted to touch.
I'm good at it. I know I'm good at it.
Is there a way to do that and actually get paid?"

That question stopped me.

Because the answer is yes.

The answer has always been yes.

But for the first time in history,
the tools that make it possible
are free, available on any smartphone,
and require no technical training to start.

This chapter is for Margaret.

And for everyone who has ever looked at
the work they do — the caregiving, the organizing,
the problem-solving, the teaching, the managing —
and wondered whether it was worth something
beyond the people it served.

It is.

And AI is the reason you can prove it.

## What AI Can Do for Your Business

Let me be specific, because this chapter
promised to be practical.

AI cannot replace your expertise.
Your decades of living, working, caring,
managing, building, surviving —
no tool can replicate that.

What AI can do is handle everything else.

The writing. The planning. The scheduling.
The research. The first draft. The follow-up.
The website copy. The email. The business plan.
The marketing strategy. The proposal.

Everything that isn't your expertise —
AI can do it. Free. Right now. Today.

Here is how.

---

## Step 1: Name What You Know

Before you open any tool, do this:

Take out a piece of paper. Write down
five things you have done in your life
that other people found hard or confusing.

Not things you were paid to do.
Things you just *did* — because you knew how,
because someone needed it done,
because you were the person in the room
who understood it.

For Margaret: managing complex medical schedules,
coordinating between multiple doctors,
translating medical language for family members,
organizing insurance paperwork.

For Robert, one of our 347 Pioneers:
running a construction crew, reading building plans,
negotiating with suppliers, managing subcontractors.

For Gloria: thirty years as a home health aide —
knowing what families in crisis need before they ask,
knowing how to calm a frightened patient,
knowing what questions to ask the doctor
when the family doesn't know what to ask.

These are not hobbies.
These are businesses waiting to happen.

The only thing that was missing was the infrastructure
to deliver them without a staff, an office,
or a decade of technical training.

That infrastructure now exists.
It fits in your pocket.
And most of it is free.

---

## Step 2: Build Your Business Plan in 30 Minutes

Open ChatGPT (free at chat.openai.com)
or Claude (free at claude.ai).

Type this:

> *"I want to start a small business helping [describe what you do].
> My target customers are [describe who needs this].
> I want to work from home, keep my costs low,
> and charge enough to make it worth my time.
> Help me build a simple one-page business plan."*

That is it.

In five minutes you will have a business plan
that would have taken a consultant two weeks
and several thousand dollars to produce.

It will tell you:
- What your service is called
- Who your customers are
- How to price it
- How to find your first clients
- What to say when someone asks what you do

Print it. Put it on the wall.
You are now a business owner.

---

## Step 3: Name Your Business and Build Your Brand

Ask the same AI:

> *"Help me come up with five names for my business.
> I want something that sounds professional,
> is easy to remember, and reflects what I do.
> Then help me write a one-sentence description
> of what I offer."*

Then:

> *"Write me a short bio I can use when I introduce myself
> at a networking event or on a website.
> I am [your age], I have [X years] of experience in [your area],
> and I am starting this business because [your reason]."*

Within twenty minutes you have:
- A business name
- A tagline
- A professional bio

The tools do not know your story.
You do.
Give them the raw material and let them
shape it into language that works.

---

## Step 4: Build Your Online Presence — Free

You do not need to hire a web designer.
You do not need to understand code.
You need two things:

**A free website:** Go to Squarespace.com or Wix.com.
Both have free plans. Both have templates
you can fill in like a form.
The AI you just used can write every word
that goes on the page.

Ask it:
> *"Write the homepage text for my [business name] website.
> Include a headline, a short description of what I do,
> three benefits for my clients, and a call to action."*

Copy. Paste. Done.

**A Google Business Profile:** Go to business.google.com.
Create a free profile for your business.
This puts you on Google Maps and Google Search —
the two places people look when they need
someone who does what you do.

You now have a website and you are on Google.
Cost: $0.
Time: one afternoon.

---

## Step 5: Find Your First Clients

The fastest path to your first paying client
is not an ad campaign or a social media strategy.

It is a list of ten people who already know you.

Ask the AI:

> *"Help me write a short message to send to
> friends and family introducing my new business.
> I want it to sound personal, not like a sales pitch.
> I want them to know what I do and who to refer me to."*

Send that message to ten people.

One of those ten people knows someone
who needs exactly what you do.

That is your first client.

From there, ask the AI to help you
write a follow-up email, a thank-you note,
a simple contract, a client intake form —
every piece of professional infrastructure
you need to run the engagement properly.

The AI writes the first draft.
You make it sound like you.
You send it.

---

## Step 6: Manage Your Business Like a CEO

Once you have clients, you need to stay organized.

Here is the free stack I recommend
for any solo business owner 50-plus:

**Google Calendar (free)** — Your schedule.
Every appointment, every deadline, every follow-up.
Ask the AI to help you build a weekly routine
that protects your working hours and your rest.

**Gmail (free)** — Your professional email.
Get a Gmail address with your business name
(yourname@gmail.com or yourname.consulting@gmail.com).
Ask the AI to help you write professional email templates
for every situation you will encounter:
new client inquiry, proposal, follow-up, invoice, thank you.

**Google Docs (free)** — Your documents.
Proposals, contracts, client notes, reports —
all in one place, accessible from any device.
Ask the AI to draft any document you need.

**Wave (free)** — Your invoicing and accounting.
Wave.com is completely free for invoicing,
expense tracking, and basic accounting.
You do not need a bookkeeper to start.

**Canva (free)** — Your visual materials.
Business cards. Flyers. Social media graphics.
Presentation slides. All free. All drag and drop.
No design experience required.

Total cost of this stack: $0.

Total capability: everything a solo professional
needs to run a legitimate, organized business.

## Step 7: Use AI Every Single Day

Here is the habit I want you to build.

Every morning, before you start your work:
Open your AI tool. Type one question.

It might be:
> *"I have a client meeting today about [topic].
> What questions should I ask to understand their needs?"*

Or:
> *"I need to follow up with a client who hasn't responded in two weeks.
> Help me write an email that is professional and not pushy."*

Or:
> *"I'm not sure how to price this project.
> Here's what's involved: [describe it].
> Help me think through a fair price."*

The AI does not replace your judgment.
It gives you a starting point.
A first draft. A framework.
Something to react to rather than create from scratch.

That is the skill.
Not knowing AI. Not understanding technology.
Knowing what question to ask.

You have spent fifty-plus years learning
to ask the right questions.
This is the same skill. New tool.

## What Margaret Did Next

Three months after that coffee conversation,
Margaret launched Care Navigator Consulting.

She helps families of adults with complex medical needs
do exactly what she spent six years doing
for her mother — understand the system,
coordinate the care, ask the right questions,
and avoid the mistakes that cost time and money
and unnecessary suffering.

She charges $75 an hour.
She has four clients.
She works twenty hours a week.

She built the business plan with ChatGPT.
She wrote the website copy with Claude.
She sends her invoices through Wave.
She manages her calendar in Google.
She writes her client communications with AI
and then adjusts them until they sound like her.

She is not a tech person.

She is a person who spent fifty years
becoming expert at something
that the world desperately needs —
and who finally found the tools
to turn that expertise into income.

*"I did it after 50," she told me last month.
"I kept hearing you say that. I decided to believe you."*

## You Already Have the Business

The business you are looking for
is not somewhere else.

It is in the decades behind you.
In the problems you have already solved.
In the skills you developed without calling them skills.
In the knowledge you accumulated without knowing
you were accumulating it.

The only thing that changes today
is the infrastructure.

AI builds the documents.
Free tools build the systems.
Your expertise builds the relationships.
Your relationships build the business.

You do not need to understand how AI works.

You need to understand what you know —
and trust that the world will pay for it.

Open one conversation.
Type one question.
Take one step.

The rest follows.
